# System design

The design of go-slow: how it models the editorial work of a slow-news briefing, and why it has this shape. Terms are defined in [`CONTEXT.md`](../../CONTEXT.md). Hypotheses are stated here without status; status is in the register, derived from readings, and until the first reading every hypothesis is Due.

## End state

**The Owner keeps reading the Briefing and the Sunday read.** Continued use is the top because the mechanism of go-slow's value is hard to pin down: whatever it is, a reader who keeps coming back is getting it, and a reader who stops is not. go-slow sits on top of the outlets it collects from, not in place of them, so the test is use, not displacement of other news. The parts below are the faster, finer readings that say why use holds or falls.

*Reading rule.* Over a rolling 8-week window, count every day a **Briefing** is opened and every **Sunday read** opened.
- **Refuted**, in any window: fewer than 2 days in 7 on average, or 3 or fewer of the 8 **Sunday reads**. Either output being abandoned refutes it, since both are the end state.
- **Supported**, only in a quiet window (commits to go-slow on no more than 1 day in 7 on average): at least 4 days in 7, and at least 6 of the 8 **Sunday reads**.
- **Undecided**: anything else.

The **Owner** is also the builder, and opens the outputs to check work as well as to read them. That error inflates use, so a busy window can refute but never support: if even the builder doesn't open the **Briefing**, that is strong evidence; if the builder does, it is weak. Separating reading from checking by excluding days with commits fails while development is daily, because it excludes nearly every day. An undecided window is re-read on the next fresh window, judged alone. Review every 8 weeks once the **Briefing** runs daily.

### Necessary conditions

Three cheap, coarse conditions for any use at all. Each runs on every change and on the running system; any failure refutes, and passing confirms nothing. Without them a broken **Collection** or a missing **Briefing** would surface weeks later, as a dip in use.

- **The outputs appear.** A **Briefing** every day and a **Sunday read** every Sunday, by a time the **Owner** sets. Refuted by any missed day.
- **Everything traces.** Every **Development** in a **Briefing** traces to at least one **Item** in the **Record**, and every **Fact** to an **Item** or a **Reference source**. Refuted by any untraced one. Statements with nothing behind them are the way AI news summaries fail in public (see [prior art](../research/5-prior-art.md)), and this is the mechanical guard against it.
- **No Source goes quiet.** Every **Source** yields new **Items** within its usual gap. Refuted by any **Source** silent past it without notice. The usual gap is declared with the **Source** when it is added, and silence runs from its newest **Item**'s publication, or from when collecting it began if it has yielded none, so a **Source** that never yields is caught too. Counting from publication rather than from when go-slow last collected means a **Collection** that has stopped running shows every **Source** quiet, which is the notice wanted. The Items page shows the flag.

### Read from Feedback

The four parts below are all read from one instrument, the **Feedback** in the **Record**, by its kind. The **Owner** gives **Feedback** in the course of reading and building anyway, so these readings cost nothing extra and carry the signal while the top can't be supported. They need no ground truth of what mattered: an outside record (a year-in-review page, an outlet's weekly roundup) is sparse, one outlet's judgement, or not licensed to keep, and the **Owner**'s **Topics** and **Depths** are the only standard of what matters here anyway. Each count is broken down by level (**Story**, **Development**, **Fact**, **Connection**), so a part that moves shows where. **Feedback** the **Owner** undid is not counted: it was a mistake in giving, and counting it would read the **Owner**'s slip as the **Editor**'s.

*Shared reading rule for the three parts that should fall.* Count **Feedback** of the part's kind per **Briefing**, from the **Record** and never by hand, over the last 4 weeks against the 4 before. **Refuted** if the rate rose; **supported** if it fell by at least a quarter; **undecided** otherwise. A fall also results from the **Owner** giving less **Feedback** at all, so these are read only while the top is not refuted. Due every 4 weeks, and on any change to the **Editor**, including to how **Feedback** is applied.

### Coverage: the Editor misses less

*Missed* **Feedback** falls: the **Stories**, **Developments** and **Connections** the **Owner** has to add become fewer.

### Brevity: the Editor includes less that isn't wanted

*Unwanted* **Feedback** falls: the **Owner** cuts less. Brevity is what the **Owner** stops cutting, not a reading-time budget, because a budget would cut what matters on a heavy day and pad a light one.

### Accuracy: the Editor gets less wrong

*Wrong* **Feedback** falls: fewer **Facts** untrue as at their date, **Claims** taken for **Facts**, or mistaken **Connections**. Because the **Record** feeds the editorial work, accuracy is what keeps the other parts from degrading as the **Record** grows.

### Foresight: the Editor shows the Owner what they hadn't seen

*New to me* **Feedback** keeps coming, mostly on **Connections** and on the **Sunday read**. This is where go-slow differs from existing news tools, none of which follows a **Story** as an ongoing subject or connects subjects by cause (see [prior art](../research/5-prior-art.md)).

*Reading rule.* Over each 4-week window, **refuted** if there is no *new to me* **Feedback**; **supported** if there is some in at least 3 of the 4 **Sunday reads**; **undecided** otherwise. Read only while the top is not refuted. Due every 4 weeks.

## Shape

The editorial work is the one core domain. Collection and storage are adapters around it (the Web apps Guide's ports and adapters): they fetch and keep material, and make no editorial judgement. The value of the project is the editorial work, so it is the part kept free of I/O and tested directly.

**An agent edits, and the Owner steers.** The **Editor** does the editorial work every day without waiting on the **Owner**, guided by the **Owner**'s **Topics** and **Depths**. The **Owner** reads its output and gives **Feedback** on it, and the **Feedback** is kept in the **Record**. Steering is **Feedback** too: telling the **Editor** a **Story** was missed or unwanted is how the **Owner** influences which **Stories** they are shown. A briefing the **Owner** had to edit would wait on the **Owner**'s time, the opposite of one they read. An **Editor** with no **Feedback** loop would let its mistakes compound, because the **Record** feeds the editorial work: later **Connections** are made over earlier **Facts**, so a wrong **Fact** left in place spreads through every **Connection** built on it. Its form is under *The Editor*.

**The editorial output is the same for every reader.** The owner's **Topics** and their **Depths** shape the editorial work, and everyone who reads the briefing reads the same one. There is no model of an audience: no per-reader selection, no segments, no engagement signal. A briefing personalised per reader would multiply the editorial work by the number of readers and turn judgement into ranking. One output keeps the editorial work the thing that is done, once.

**go-slow is private: the Owner is its only reader.** It is not publicly reachable. Publishers' terms and UK copyright law draw their sharpest line between private, personal, non-commercial use and publishing to others, so a private go-slow can use the widest set of **Sources** and keep the most of each. Opening it to other readers changes nothing in the editorial design, since the output is already the same for every reader, but it changes what the **Record** may hold and which **Sources** may be collected, and is made only once licensing allows it.

**go-slow runs when the Owner starts it.** It runs on the **Owner**'s laptop, from the same image and compose file it is developed with, and its port is published on the loopback interface only, so it stays private on any network the laptop joins: a port open on every interface would serve the **Record** to everyone else on a shared network. The **Record** is an ordinary folder on the laptop, mounted into the container, so the **Owner** can see it, copy it and keep it across resets of Docker. Nothing keeps go-slow running when the **Owner** hasn't started it, because starting it is reading it: the **Owner** starts it each day to read, and a host running it unattended would write **Briefings** nobody opens, which nothing in the end state counts. A day it isn't started is a day not read, and the top's reading already counts it as one, so it needs no rule of its own. Under *The outputs appear* as it stands, a day it isn't started has no **Briefing** by the set time and is a missed day, and so is a day it is started after the set time; whether that condition should count only the days go-slow ran is the **Owner**'s review, and until then its rule stands.

## The Editor

**The Editor's daily work is a sequence of editorial acts.** Each act is a bounded step with a typed result, reading a slice of the **Record** and writing back only what passes its checks:

1. *Reading an Item* draws its **Facts** and **Claims**, and the **Entities** the **Item** mentions.
2. *Placing a Fact* judges which **Stories** it moves on, making it a **Development** in each, or none.
3. *Connecting* makes **Connections** between **Facts**: cause, and shared **Entities** in their roles.
4. *Writing the Briefing* turns the day's **Developments** in the **Owner**'s **Stories** into the **Briefing**.

An act is a model call where judgement is needed and plain code where it is not. A single agent with tools over the **Record**, deciding for itself what to do each day, fails on four counts. **Feedback** lands on an act: *missed* and *unwanted* on a **Development** point at placing, *wrong* on a **Fact** at reading, *wrong* on a **Connection** at connecting, so when a part of the end state worsens the act to fix is known; with one agent, only that the agent got worse. "Everything traces" becomes a property of each act's result (a **Fact** carries its **Item**) rather than something an agent must remember. Each act can be tested first, on recorded **Items**, by properties such as "a **Claim** keeps its attribution" and "a reaction is not a **Development**". And the work grows with the number of **Items**, not with how far an agent chooses to explore.

**Reading is lazy.** Every **Item** is collected and kept, which is cheap; reading one is a model call, which is not, and most **Items** warrant nothing. An **Item** is read only when a cheap check with no model in it ties it to a **Topic**, by its **Source**, its keywords or the **Entities** its metadata names. **Items** that touch no **Topic** wait in the **Record** unread, and when the **Owner** takes up or deepens a **Topic**, the waiting **Items** that match it are tied to it then and read on the next **Collection** run, with the new ones, so taking up a **Topic** never waits on a model call. Keeping everything is what makes interest that changes recoverable; lazy reading is what makes keeping everything affordable. When the check passes over an **Item** that mattered, *missed* **Feedback** shows it, and points at the check rather than at placing.

**The check ties by Source and by terms, never by Depth.** A **Topic** names the **Sources** all of whose **Items** concern it (a department's every release, for a **Topic** about its brief) and a list of terms: keywords, and the names of the **Entities** it is about. An **Item** is tied when it comes from one of those **Sources**, or when a term appears in its title, summary or text as whole words, ignoring case and punctuation, so "Farage" ties "Mr Farage's statement" but not "Farageist". Whole words keep a short name from matching inside longer ones; anything looser (stems, synonyms, the meaning of a passage) is judgement, which belongs to *reading*, not to a check that runs over everything collected. Until a **Source** carries **Entity** names in its own metadata, the names are matched in the text. A **Topic** must name at least one term or **Source**, since one with neither could tie nothing. **Depth** decides how much of what is read matters, not whether an **Item** is read, so the check ignores it: an **Item** is either worth reading for a **Topic** or not, and how closely the **Owner** follows that **Topic** shapes what reading and placing keep. Taking up a **Topic** re-runs the check for it over the **Items** waiting unread; removing one drops its ties, and its **Items** wait again unless another **Topic** ties them.

**Depth is an ordered scale of three named levels**: *passing* (only what moves the **Topic** on in a big way), *following* (its **Developments** as they happen) and *close* (the detail as well, reactions and all). Named levels give each act an instruction it can follow and give **Feedback** a **Depth** to be read against (*an unwanted reaction at one Depth is not unwanted at every Depth*); a free number would leave every act to guess what 0.6 means, and a scale with more levels would ask the **Owner** to make distinctions no act can honour. Being ordered, "deepening" a **Topic** means moving it up the scale.

**The model is an adapter, and the one adapter is Claude.** Every act that calls a model does so through a provider-neutral port, and each act owns its instructions and output schema, so the acts are tested against a fake and each act's model is a configuration value. The one adapter is Claude through Anthropic's API, and which Claude model runs which act is chosen on the recorded fixtures by their properties ([model research](../research/models.md)). Open-weight models are not used. From an inference provider they are paid per token like Claude, and at go-slow's volume (about 100 daily calls) every candidate costs a few dollars a month, so the most they could save is less than a second adapter, a second set of recordings for every act and a measurement between providers cost to build and keep. Where they would be free at the margin, a model the **Owner** runs, they need a machine running all day, and the models such a machine holds are not the frontier ones. The port stays provider-neutral, so a second adapter stays a new integration rather than a redesign, if the cost of the calls ever outgrows the cost of the work. Each act runs on a pinned model ID, one whose weights the provider never changes, so behaviour can't change between two readings, or under a green recording, without a change in go-slow; an alias the provider moves to a newer model would change the act while every recording still passed. A model's ID is part of the act's request, so changing it is a re-record, like changing the act's instructions. Each result keeps the model the provider reports answering with, not the one asked for, so if a configured ID ever did move, the **Record** would show it.

**A provider never trains on go-slow's requests and keeps them only for a limited, stated period.** Most of what is sent is public, but every request carries the **Owner**'s **Topics**, **Depths** and **Feedback** as precedent, which together are a profile of what the **Owner** follows. Strict zero retention would shut out most closed models' standard terms for little gain over a profile of interests; a provider that trains on requests or keeps them indefinitely is excluded.

**The daily acts run in batches; the Briefing is written in real time.** Nothing waits on a single **Item**, and the design already accepts up to a **Collection** run between an **Item** arriving and its **Development** appearing, so the daily acts are submitted together and processed asynchronously, which is cheaper where a provider discounts batches, as Anthropic does. Writing the **Briefing** is one request just before it is due, so it runs at once. A batch is never waited on: after each **Collection** run the results of batches that have ended are applied, through each act's own checks, and then everything awaiting an act and not already in a batch is submitted as one batch. Results are matched to their work by the identifier each request was given, never by position, since a provider returns them in any order. Work stays awaiting its act until its result is written, and the pending batches are kept in the **Record**, so a restart picks them up and a result is applied to the work as it stands when it arrives. A request that failed, expired or fails its act's checks is resubmitted alone, so one bad **Item** confines its failure to itself, as *The acts run as Items arrive* requires, rather than waiting for the next batch or holding one up.

**Started late, the Briefing waits for the start's work.** Nothing is collected while go-slow is stopped, so a morning start collects everything since the last run, and its reading, placing and connecting go out in batches, one act after another. Past the set time, the **Briefing** is written once nothing collected before it began waiting still awaits an act, in a pending batch or not; work marked failed awaits nothing. It begins waiting at its first chance past the set time, and never before the start's first **Collection** run has ended, so that run's **Items** are waited for. Writing it at once would tell yesterday's work and push the overnight **Developments** into the next day's **Briefing**; running the start's acts in real time instead would give up the batch discount, and the **Owner** prefers the wait. **Items** collected while it waits do not hold it, or a steady trickle of them could hold it all morning. The wait is bounded at a few hours, so a batch that never ends cannot keep the **Briefing** from being written that morning; the batch deadline would not do as the bound, since at two days it outlasts the day the **Briefing** is for. Until it is written, the day's page says it is being written and polls for it. On Sundays the **Sunday read** waits with it, and follows it as always.

**Work that keeps failing is given up after three attempts, and taken up again by hand.** Each piece of work (one act on one **Item**) gets three attempts across its batches, the first and each resubmission alike; after the third failure it is marked failed in the **Record**, with when and why it last failed, and is no longer submitted. A batch that has not ended, or cannot be polled, two days after it was submitted (twice the day within which a provider ends one) is given up, and counts a failed attempt for each piece of work in it, so a batch that disappears at the provider or never ends doesn't leave its work pending for good. The cap is there because every attempt is a model call: a request whose answer always fails its checks, or is always refused, would otherwise be paid for on every **Collection** run without bound, and a failure that persists across three attempts, minutes to hours apart, is one in the **Item** or the act, not in the provider, so a fourth attempt is unlikely to fare better. Failed work no longer awaits its act, so nothing waits behind it: an **Item** placing holds back behind an earlier one mentioning an **Entity** it mentions goes ahead once that one's placing has failed, since a failed placing starts or moves on no **Story**. Failed work is listed on the **Items** page with its reason rather than dropped silently, since an **Item** never read or placed is a gap in the **Record** the **Owner** should know of. Retrying is by hand, through the `retry` admin command (one act's failed work, or all of it), which clears the failed marks and resets the attempts: what fixes failed work is a change the system can't see (an act's instructions or checks, a provider's outage, a malformed **Item** corrected at its **Source**), so retrying on a timer would only spend the calls the cap saves, and the **Owner** is the one who knows the cause has been dealt with.

**Every model call is kept, as it was sent and as it came back.** Each call an act makes (a request in a batch, a real-time call, a turn of the **Sunday read**'s conversation) is kept with the act and the piece of work it was for, the request exactly as sent (instructions, schema, input, the precedents, candidate **Stories** or neighbouring **Facts** it was shown, the model asked for), the response exactly as returned (thinking and refusals included, in the provider's own form, beside the model the provider reports, the usage and why it stopped), and what the act's checks said of it. A result the checks refused is a kept call like any other, with its reason; a request in a batch that ended without answering it is kept with no response. This is for diagnosis: **Feedback** already says which act to blame (*Feedback lands on an act*, under *The Editor's daily work*), and the kept call says why that act judged as it did, from what it was shown rather than from a reconstruction. **Feedback** can come weeks after the call it points at, so calls are kept for good and never rewritten, like **Feedback** as given. Each carries a hash of its request (instructions, schema, input and model), computed as recorded runs compute theirs, so a call in use and a recording share one meaning of "the same request": whether today's code would still send it is a comparison, and a call that went wrong is already a fixture's input, since every **Source** is admissible. The request ties a call to the code that made it better than a commit would: the precedents and candidates depend on the **Record** as it stood, and a commit can't say what a build from uncommitted code sent. The kept calls are a side record, like the opens and commits the register keeps: no act is shown them, they are never precedent and **Feedback** is not given on them, since an act shown its own earlier answers would be taking instructions by another route (the Model calls Guide). Usage is shown as tokens, summed per page, not as money: prices change, and a price table in the code would go stale unseen.

**How each thing was made is one click from it.** A **Fact** links to the reading that drew it, a **Development** to the placing that made it, a **Connection** to the connecting, an **Assessment** to the **Sunday read**'s conversation; each call's page shows its request, its response, what the checks said, and every other attempt at that piece of work. The **Briefing**'s page and the **Sunday read**'s link to how each was written: its own call or conversation, turn by turn, and the placing and reading calls behind the **Developments** it cites, with their tokens. The **Briefing**'s is followed as it was written: **Feedback** that later took a statement out does not change what went into writing it. The **Sunday read**'s is its conversation's page, since the conversation is the whole of how the read was written: its tools read the **Record** directly, not through other calls. The Feedback page links each piece to the call behind what it is about (a **Story**'s are the placing calls of its **Developments**), worked out by the same lookups as the things' own pages rather than stored with the piece, so the two can't disagree. What *missed* added, and **Feedback** on it, links to no call and says the **Owner** made it: a blank there would read as a call lost. Diagnosis starts from **Feedback**, which is given on a **Fact**, **Development**, **Connection** or **Assessment**, usually acts upstream of the **Briefing**, so a page per output alone would leave the trail to be rebuilt by clicking back through it. These pages are checking work, not reading, so opening them is no open (*The Owner keeps reading*).

**The Editor is tested in three tiers.** Unit tests replace the model with a fake, injected rather than patched, and test first everything around the model call: typed results, the checks before writing back, tracing, and **Feedback**'s fixes to the **Record**. Recorded runs call the real model on fixture **Items**, keep its responses, and replay them in the suite, asserting properties (every **Fact** traces to its **Item**, a **Claim** keeps its attribution, a reaction is not a **Development**, a speech's **Facts** come from its primary text); a property that rests on **Feedback** names a **Feedback** fixture, made by hand from an admissible **Item**, never from a model's earlier output (a reaction like one marked *unwanted* at the **Owner**'s **Depth** is not a **Development**; an estimate like a forecast marked *wrong* is not drawn as a **Fact**); they are re-recorded deliberately when an act's instructions change, and the difference shows what changed. A replay is valid only for the request it answered. Each recording keeps that request's hash: its instructions, schema and input (frozen inputs included), and the model the act is pinned to, by the one function a kept call is hashed with (for the Sunday read, one hash per turn). A replay whose act now builds a different request fails as stale, naming the command that re-records it. Without this, a change to an act's instructions leaves its old recordings green while they test a prompt that no longer exists. It fails rather than skips, because a skipped property quietly tests nothing, and a recording that keeps no hash is stale too. A turn of the **Sunday read** is hashed over its request exactly as sent, the model's earlier turns in the provider's own form, and a recording keeps each turn in that form and replays it, so the act passes back what came and the replay sends the request the recording answered. Hashing a neutral form (the turn's text and tool calls alone) would also make a recorded and a kept hash agree, but it would leave out what the provider is sent of the model's own turn, its thinking among it, so two requests the model is asked differently would hash the same, and every hash already kept would change its meaning. Whether the **Editor** judges as the **Owner** would is not a test but a reading, through **Feedback** in use. So the suite never pays for a model call; only a deliberate re-recording does. Fixtures come only from admissible **Sources**, since the suite is committed.

**Each act is recorded over its own frozen inputs.** Each act is recorded over its own frozen copies of the recordings it rests on (placing over readings, connecting over readings and placings, the Briefing and the Sunday read over all three), never over the upstream recordings themselves, so re-recording one act leaves every other act's recordings valid, and a change to an act is re-recorded on that act alone. The copies are earlier acts' recordings, made by the production path, not hand-made **Record** fixtures: hand-made **Facts** would let placing pass on input no reading writes. A frozen input is part of the act's request, so refreshing it makes the act's recording stale. Refreshing is a change of its own, never part of the change to the act upstream: that act's new behaviour is accepted on its own recordings first, and then the copies are refreshed and what rests on them re-recorded, once, rather than once per fix. A refresh copies every upstream act's recordings together, because a downstream act frozen on new readings and old placings would be tested on a mix no run of the **Editor** produces. Until a refresh, the acts downstream are tested on what the earlier acts used to write. Two checks cover that gap, neither needing a recording of its own. The chain as a whole is replayed over the Briefing's frozen copies and each of its samples, each one consistent run, and asserts only what spans acts: each statement of the Briefing traces through a **Development** and a **Fact** to a quote in its **Item**; a separately recorded chain would be one more set of recordings to keep in step, doing the same. And each act's current recordings (each fixture's first sample) are fed to the acts downstream with a fake model, asserting that their requests build and the fake's answers pass their checks, so a change to an earlier act's output that a later act can no longer take fails at once. A change in what the earlier act judges, rather than in what shape it writes, is not a failure of the code; a refresh is what brings it downstream. The Sunday read is refreshed only on the **Owner**'s say, since its re-recording costs dollars.

**A recording is one sample, and a property is judged by its pass rate.** A recorded act is stochastic, so one recording passing says only that it passed once: in one re-recording, a speech's **Facts** came from its primary text in one try of three, and the earlier recording's pass had been luck that nothing could have shown. So each fixture is sampled several times when it is recorded, and each property is judged by the rate at which the samples pass it. A sample is one piece of work run as it runs in use, with its attempts against the act's own checks (`MaxAttempts`), and the two kinds of property are rated apart, because they fail differently. An act's checks run in use, so a check that refuses a call costs a retry, not a wrong **Record**: the property is that the work passes within its attempts. The properties only the suite asserts (a speech's **Facts** come from its primary text, a reaction is not a **Development**) run nowhere in use, so each failure is a wrong **Record**: they are rated over the samples that passed their checks, each call counting. Rating both per call would treat a check that costs a retry one time in three like a property that is wrong one time in three. Each property is judged per fixture, not pooled across them, because a property can fail on one **Item** (the speech) while the rate across all of them looks fine. The rule is declared before the samples are drawn. Reading, placing, connecting and the Briefing take five samples of each fixture: a property is supported at five of five, refuted at three or fewer, and undecided at four, which is read again on five fresh samples judged alone, never added to the first five. The Sunday read, at dollars a run, takes three: supported at three, refuted at one or none, undecided at two. Its sample is the read as written in use: up to `SundayReadTries` tries, each a whole conversation over a fresh copy of its frozen **Record**, kept with Wikidata's answers to its lookups, so a replay makes no network call. Each try starts from the same **Record**, so the samples share their opening turn's request; the turns after it differ with what each conversation did. No source gives these lines; they are coarse on purpose, since five samples cannot tell a rate of 80% from one of 95%, and what five of five says is only that nothing is wrong that a necessary condition can see. Every sample is committed, and the suite replays them all and judges each property by its rule on every run, so a property's status is derived from the recordings, never stored, like the register's. A rate written only into a change's description would be a claim nothing checks again, and a later change to how a property is asserted would quietly leave the samples it was rated on. A refuted or undecided property is a failing test: a refuted one until the act is changed, an undecided one until its fresh samples replace the first ones. The samples answer one request, so they share its hash. Where an act's fixtures build on each other (placing and connecting run over the fixtures in the order they were published, so later **Facts** meet what earlier ones made), every sample of a fixture runs over the same **Record**: the one the fixtures before it left, each carried on by its first sample to pass its checks. A sample run over the **Record** its own earlier samples made would send a request of its own, and five such samples would be five readings of one each, not one request's rate. A frozen copy of an act's recording, for an act downstream, is its first sample, chosen before its results are seen; copies are refreshed only from recordings whose properties are supported, so that sample passed. Recorded properties are not hypotheses of the end state: they say an act behaves on fixtures, not that the **Owner** keeps reading, and their status is the suite's, not the register's. Where one serves a part of the end state it is that part's test on fixtures (the chain replay is *Everything traces* before the **Record** exists), and the part is still read from use. Whether the **Editor** judges as the **Owner** would stays the third tier, read from **Feedback**.

**Entities come from Wikidata first, otherwise from the Editor.** *Reading an Item* looks each person, organisation, place or named occasion up in Wikidata and uses a match as it is. With no match, the **Editor** makes its own **Entity** (a would-be candidate, a councillor, a new campaign group); if Wikidata later gains an item for it, the two are merged and the **Editor**'s **Entity** stays as an alias, so the **Record**'s history still resolves. Wikidata gives identity, so "Andy Burnham", "the Prime Minister" and "Burnham" in three **Items** are one **Entity** without go-slow solving that matching itself, and it gives dated facts about **Entities** (a position held since a date) that become **Facts** traced to a **Reference source**. Abstract subjects are **Topics**, not **Entities**: an **Entity** is made only for something a **Fact** involves in a role.

**The model names Entities as the Item does; plain code finds them in Wikidata.** *Reading an Item* gives each **Entity** the name the **Item** uses, its kind, and for a person the office the **Item** gives them, in full ("the Prime Minister", role *Prime Minister of the United Kingdom*). Only a person has a role, and code drops any role the model gives an organisation, place or occasion, keeping the **Entity** with its name and kind and refusing nothing. The role is used only to find a person among an office's holders, so another kind's role has no use, and the model fills the field for other kinds whenever anything near it describes what an **Entity** does (a definition of what a **Fact** involves, say), on most mentions; instructions can't hold it off, while code drops it whatever the model writes, and leaves reading's request unchanged. It gives them once, as what the **Item** mentions: every person, organisation, place and named occasion the **Item** names, apart from its **Facts** and **Claims**, and the **Record** keeps them as the **Item**'s mentions. Nothing refers into the list, since which of them a **Fact** involves is connecting's judgement (*A Fact involves an Entity when the Entity has a role in it*), so no statement can name an **Entity** the list doesn't describe. Asking reading for a list with each **Fact** and **Claim** as well would have it judge involvement, which it does by a standard of its own and differently from sample to sample, and a list the statements referred into by name would fail the reading, a paid call each time, whenever a statement named an **Entity** the list never gave. The check keeps one name one thing: a reading that gives one name two kinds is refused, while a name given twice with different roles keeps the first it is given (an empty role is filled by a later one), rather than being refused, since a person can hold more than one office and one is enough to find them among its holders. What earlier readings gave with each **Fact** and **Claim** is, together, what their **Item** mentions, and is kept as its mentions. Matching is then plain code over Wikidata's answers, as at the **Item**'s date. A person with a role is sought first among the role's holders on that date (Wikidata's officeholder statements with their start and end): the one holder, when the **Item** names them only by the role, or the holder whose name contains the **Item**'s ("Burnham"). Otherwise the match is the best item Wikidata's own search ranks whose label or an alias is the name, and of the right kind (a person is an instance of *human*, nothing else is). Anything less certain is no match, and the **Editor** makes its own **Entity**. Letting the model say who "the Prime Minister" is would draw identity from its recall, which is untraced and goes stale exactly where news happens (a model trained before 20 July 2026 names the wrong Prime Minister); a role resolved by date against Wikidata is right for an **Item** from any year. Search alone fails the other way: "Burnham" finds villages before people. An Editor's **Entity** is merged into a Wikidata item when a later reading matches that item under the same name, or the item's label, so the merge needs no sweep of its own. A failed lookup writes nothing, like a failed check, and the reading is applied again once Wikidata answers.

**A Fact involves an Entity when the Entity has a role in it.** The roles are connecting's four: who or what did it (*actor*), what or whom it was done to, or what it says something of (*undergoer*), where (*place*) and at what named occasion (*occasion*). *Actor* and *undergoer* are Role and Reference Grammar's two macroroles, each a generalisation across the finer roles: undergoer subsumes patient, theme and recipient, and is the one role of a state ("the Becketwell site is brownfield") or of an intransitive like *die* (Van Valin, *Semantic macroroles in Role and Reference Grammar*, read in full). The pair is the coarsest grain at which roles still say who did what to whom, and coarse roles are the ones annotators agree on (PropBank's κ is about .93), while finer, event-specific ones fall well below it ([news content research](../research/92-news-content-modelling.md)). *Undergoer* says nothing of harm or opposition, which *affected* or *target* would ("those affected by the floods"), so a minister appointed, or a council in a partnership, is labelled without a judgement the **Fact** doesn't make. Nor does it ask what a statement is about in the sense of what its **Item** is about: that judgement, aboutness, is the least reliable the research found, and asking for it draws in whatever is salient in the **Item**. Which **Entities** a **Fact** involves, and in what role, is one judgement, made by connecting alone: it is shown every **Entity** the **Item** mentions, once, and chooses from them for each **Fact**, rather than giving roles to lists *reading an Item* made per **Fact**. Lists made per **Fact** by reading vary from sample to sample, and by a standard of their own: the model reading an **Item** narrows "involves" to who acts or speaks, leaving out what a statement is about ("made a statement on the Great British Railways headquarters" lists only the speaker) and where it happened, and lists names with no role in the statement. A judgement split across two acts varies with both, and the act shown a list judges the list rather than the **Fact**. Reading gives the **Entities** the **Item** mentions once, apart from its **Facts** and **Claims** (*The model names Entities as the Item does*). A name in a statement with no role in it is not involved: in "the Prime Minister met President Zelenskyy of Ukraine", Ukraine says who Zelenskyy is, and the **Fact** involves it only if it says something of Ukraine; in "the GBR headquarters site at Becketwell is brownfield", the site is the undergoer and Great British Railways only says whose site it is. This is the line between an argument and a modifier inside it, which every role scheme draws, rather than one of go-slow's own; what it costs (a **Fact** about GBR's headquarters does not connect GBR's **Stories** through GBR) is small because placing finds those **Stories** by what the **Item** mentions (*What finds work rests on mentions*). So which **Entities** a **Fact** involves is judgement, not plain code: a check that every **Entity** a statement names is involved refuses readings over names with no role, and code that added them would leave connecting to invent one. Connecting makes it, since roles are its job: a **Fact** involves the **Entities** connecting gives a role in it, and no other (*Connecting weighs an Item's Facts against the Facts near them*). Who made a **Claim** is its attribution, kept with it; the **Claim** involves them only if it gives them a role.

**A reciprocal act has two actors.** An act the **Fact** states as mutual (they met, spoke, agreed together) has each party as an *actor*; an act with a direction (urged, assured, welcomed, offered) has its doer as *actor* and the one it was directed at as *undergoer*. A collective is one *actor* only where the **Item** names it as one (the G7, the signatories of a joint declaration); two **Entities** are never made into a joint actor. Both parties are actors, not actor and undergoer by the statement's grammar, because an **Item** about a meeting is usually one government's readout, and its framing ("the Prime Minister spoke to President Zelenskyy") is that government's account, not the event's structure: reading the subject as the actor would make the other party's **Stories** connect to the meeting only as something done to them, and the same call read from Kyiv's readout would swap the two. The line between mutual and directed acts matters as much, because most acts in readouts have a direction (one side welcomes, urges, assures or offers), and calling them all mutual would make *actor* mean only "was there". Two **Stories** connected by an **Entity** in the same role, and the **Sunday read**'s ties through the same **Entity** in the same role, rely on *actor* meaning who did it: "was there" would tie every meeting a visiting leader attended to everything the host did. A joint actor made from two **Entities** ("the Prime Minister and President Zelenskyy") is no **Entity** at all, so it identifies nothing to connect through; the check already refuses it as a name the **Item**'s reading never gave, and only a collective the **Item** names (and reading gives as an **Entity**) can be one actor. No check limits a **Fact** to one actor.

**What finds work rests on mentions; what links Stories rests on involvement.** An **Item** mentions every **Entity** it names, and a **Fact** involves only those with a role in it, so the two relations carry different weight. Finding what a **Fact** is weighed against (placing's candidate **Stories**, connecting's nearby **Facts**, the **Facts** offered as a **Development** a **Story** missed) rests on mentions: a miss there starts a duplicate **Story** or loses a cause, and what an **Item** names is the judgement humans and models make most reliably, while which of the named things a statement involves, and in what role, is among the least reliable ([news content research](../research/92-news-content-modelling.md)). Saying that two **Stories** are connected, and the **Sunday read**'s **Facts** involving an **Entity**, rest on involvement in a role: there a false tie misleads the **Owner**, and a missed one costs only a missing link. So "The GBR headquarters site at Becketwell is brownfield" makes Great British Railways' **Stories** candidates for placing it whether or not the **Fact** is judged to involve GBR, and only the connection between **Stories** waits on that judgement. Resting everything on involvement would let the least reliable judgement decide what is even weighed; resting everything on mentions would connect two **Stories** whenever their **Items** both name the Prime Minister. Lazy reading's check is neither: it matches terms in the text, with no model.

**A position held becomes a Fact only to the day.** For a matched person, each position Wikidata says they hold on the **Item**'s date, with a start given to the day, becomes a **Fact** ("Andy Burnham took office as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom on 20 July 2026"), holding as at its start and traced to the lookup: the item and the revision of it seen, under Wikidata's CC0 licence ([sources research](../research/2-sources.md)). A start known only to the year would make a **Fact** claim a day no source gives. The same **Fact** found by a later lookup is kept once.

**Placing weighs a Fact against the Stories its Item's Entities are in.** The candidates for a new **Fact** are the **Stories** with a **Development** whose **Item** mentions an **Entity** its own **Item** mentions; when none fits, placing starts a new **Story**. Placing is shown at most 20, ranked by the **Entities** each shares with the **Item**, each **Entity** weighted by how few **Stories** it is in (inverse document frequency, over **Stories**), and by its newest **Development** where they tie. Every **Fact** of an **Item** has the same candidates, since they come from what the **Item** mentions. Without a cap an **Entity** in nearly everything (the Prime Minister) would make every **Story** a candidate and the call's cost grow with the **Record**; weighting by rarity is what keeps the cap from dropping the **Story** that matters, since a **Story** sharing Great British Railways outranks one sharing only the Prime Minister, months after it last moved. Ranking by recency instead would drop a quiet **Story** just when it revives (below). The ranking rests on identity, so each candidate is there for a reason that can be named (the **Entities** it shares), kept with the call like everything placing is shown. Ranking by the similarity of texts (embeddings) would find a **Story** that shares a subject but no **Entity**, which the **Entities** miss, but gives a score with no reason behind it, takes another happening of the same kind (another resignation) for the same one, and needs a model call from a provider of its own. A *missed* **Development** in a **Story** that was not among the candidates points at this ranking, not at placing's judgement: if such **Stories** keep sharing no **Entity** with the **Item**, text similarity joins the **Entities** as a second signal, rather than replacing them. **Stories** never close: one goes quiet when nothing moves it on, and the **Briefing** shows a **Story** on a day it has a **Development**. Narrowing by recency instead (only **Stories** moved on in recent weeks) would start a duplicate whenever a quiet **Story** revived, cutting the **Record**'s threads exactly where wider stories and the story behind the story need them, and would need a judgement of when a **Story** ends that nothing else requires. An **Item**'s **Facts** are placed together, in one call, so that two **Facts** from one **Item** can start one **Story** between them, and the act's check refuses the whole result if any **Fact** is left unplaced or placed in a **Story** that is not one of the **Item**'s candidates, or if a new **Story** is started by no **Fact** that fits no candidate. In a batch, an **Item** waits for the next one while an earlier **Item** awaiting placing mentions an **Entity** it mentions, since placing that **Item** may start or move on a **Story** it would be weighed against; **Items** that share no **Entity** cannot affect each other's candidates, so they go together. A batch then gives the same **Stories** as placing one **Item** at a time in the order they were published, and submitting every **Item** at once would start a duplicate **Story** whenever two **Items** in one batch began the same subject.

**Connecting weighs an Item's Facts against the Facts near them.** Connecting waits until a **Fact** is placed, then connects an **Item**'s **Facts** together, in one call, so a cause between two **Facts** of one **Item** is seen. They are weighed against the 30 other **Facts** whose **Items** mention an **Entity** theirs mentions or that are **Developments** in one of their **Stories**, those nearest them in date first. Weighing against every **Fact** that shares an **Entity** would grow with the **Record** without bound, since the Prime Minister is in nearly everything; a cause is near its effect in time far more often than not, and a cause further back is reached through the **Stories** both are in and by the **Sunday read**. The act does two things. It decides which of the **Entities** the **Item** mentions each **Fact** involves, and gives each its role in it, one of *actor*, *undergoer*, *place* and *occasion* (*A Fact involves an Entity when the Entity has a role in it*); a **Fact** may involve none. *Reading an Item* names the **Entities** and, for a person, their office, which is identity; the role is what the **Fact** says of the **Entity**, which is what lets two **Stories** be connected by an **Entity** in the same role. A closed set of roles is what makes "the same role" a comparison plain code can make; free-text roles would need a model to say whether "investigator" and "carried out the investigation" match. And it makes cause **Connections**: one **Fact** caused or led to another, at least one of them the **Item**'s own. Cause is the one kind of **Connection** between **Facts** so far, kept with its kind so others can join it. Connecting is shown each **Fact** by its statement alone, and the **Item**'s mentions once, each with its kind: every **Entity** the **Item**'s reading gave. Its schema offers only those names, so it never names one the reading didn't give. Showing lists per **Fact** instead would anchor the judgement on them, and their variation from sample to sample would pass straight into which **Entities** a **Fact** involves; the act is judged, in its recordings, by how much better its samples agree on what each **Fact** involves than reading's lists do, and if they agree no better the variation has only moved. Connecting a **Fact** again (before the **Briefing**) decides its involvement afresh: an **Entity** given no role this time is involved no more, though it stays mentioned. The act's check refuses the whole result if any **Fact** is left unconnected, an **Entity** is given two roles in one **Fact**, a role is given to a name the **Item**'s reading never gave (a stray name, which identifies no **Entity** of the **Item**), a role is not one of the four, or a cause is not between two different **Facts** shown, one of them the **Item**'s. An **Entity** the **Item** mentions given no role in a **Fact** is not refused: it is connecting's judgement that the **Fact** does not involve it. **Items** in one batch don't wait for each other, unlike placing: every read **Fact** is already in the **Record**, so each **Item** is weighed against the others either way, and a cause found from both sides is kept once.

**The Briefing tells each Development once, in statements that cite it.** A Briefing covers the **Developments** placed since the previous **Briefing** was written (the first, the day before it), so every **Development** is in exactly one **Briefing** however **Collection** runs fall around the set time; taking "the day" as the calendar day of a **Fact**'s date would drop a **Development** placed late in the day after the **Briefing**, or one whose **Item** was read days after it was published. The **Owner**'s **Topics** select the **Stories**: a **Development** is selected when its **Item** is tied to a **Topic**, and a **Story** is in the **Briefing** with only its selected **Developments**, at the closest **Depth** of the **Topics** that tie them. **Depth** shapes what is told, and the act's check enforces the part code can: at *following* and *close* every selected **Development** is cited, while at *passing* the **Editor** may leave out a **Development**, or a whole **Story**, that does not move it on in a big way, so leaving out is a judgement only where **Depth** allows one. Placing has already kept reactions out of the **Developments**, so what *close* adds here is the detail of each. The **Editor** writes only the statements and the order of the **Stories**; each statement carries the refs of the **Developments** it rests on, enumerated in the output schema, and the check refuses the whole **Briefing** if any statement cites none, or cites one not given or of another **Story**. Tracing is then a check in code on structured citations, where tracing prose to its sources would need another model call. A refused or failed **Briefing** writes nothing; the failure is kept and shown on the day's page, and writing is tried again after a quarter of an hour. With nothing selected, the **Briefing** is written empty and says so, with no model call. **The outputs appear** is checked on the running system: a day with no **Briefing** by the set time is flagged on its page and in the list of **Briefings**, from the first day a **Briefing** was written or tried.

**The acts run as Items arrive.** After each **Collection** run, each new **Item** that touches a **Topic** is read and its **Facts** placed and connected; the **Briefing** is written on its own schedule from what the **Record** holds then. That keeps the **Record** current within a **Collection** run of an **Item** arriving, confines a failure (a malformed **Item**, a failed model call) to one act on one **Item**, retried without holding up the **Briefing**, and lets a **Fact** placed later in the day see the **Stories** earlier **Facts** started. A single batch before the **Briefing** would put all of the day's work, and all of its failures, in the hour before the **Briefing** is due. Because a **Fact** placed early can't see a **Story** started later, or the **Facts** that arrive after it, *connecting* runs again over the day's **Facts** (those placed in the day before it, since the **Briefing** covers what was placed since the previous one) before the **Briefing** is written. The re-run only adds: a **Connection** made earlier stays until **Feedback** withdraws it, since the **Record** is never rewritten. It runs in real time, one call per **Item**, because the **Briefing** is due at once and can't wait on a batch.

**Feedback fixes the Record and becomes precedent.** Each piece of **Feedback** first acts on the **Record**: *missed* adds what was missing, *unwanted* takes it out of the **Briefing**, *wrong* withdraws the **Fact**, **Connection** or **Assessment** and whatever was built on it, and *new to me* changes nothing. A wrong **Fact** left in place would spread through every **Connection** made over it, so this part is not optional. Then, when an act judges, it is shown the past **Feedback** relevant to the case, found by the same **Entities** and **Topics**, with the **Depth** it was given at: an *unwanted* reaction at one **Depth** on a **Topic** is not unwanted at every **Depth**. Precedent takes effect the day **Feedback** is given and keeps the reason for a judgement visible in what the act was shown. Editing the acts' instructions by hand would make the **Owner** the **Editor** again; retraining a model on the **Feedback** would be slow to take effect and opaque about why the **Editor** changed.

**Placing is shown the Feedback on Developments near the Facts it places.** A piece of **Feedback** on a **Development** is a precedent for placing an **Item**'s **Facts** when the **Development**'s **Item** mentions an **Entity** theirs mentions, or shares a **Topic** with it: the neighbourhood placing already weighs a **Fact** in, widened by **Topic**, since a **Topic** is how the **Owner** says what kind of thing matters. It rests on mentions, as finding candidate **Stories** does (*What finds work rests on mentions*): a **Fact** waiting to be placed involves no **Entity** yet, since connecting gives roles only after placing, so matching on involvement would find precedents by **Topic** alone. Placing is shown at most eight, those sharing the most **Entities** and **Topics** first, then the newest, each with its **Story**, the **Development**'s **Fact** and the **Depth** it was given at, and is told the **Depth** the **Item**'s **Topics** are followed at now. A precedent holds at its own **Depth**, and beyond it only in its own direction: *unwanted* at any **Depth** less close (what was too much detail at *following* is too much at *passing*), *missed* at any closer. A cap keeps each call small and its cost flat as **Feedback** accumulates; without one, an **Entity** in nearly everything (the Prime Minister) would bring all the **Feedback** ever given into every call. The precedents shown are kept with the placing's result, so why the **Editor** judged as it did can be read later from what it was shown, not reconstructed from **Feedback** given since. They come after the act's instructions and schema, as data (one JSON object per line) the instructions say are never instructions, so the instructions stay a stable prefix a provider can cache, and nothing the **Owner** typed or an **Item** said is read as an instruction. **Feedback** on a withdrawn **Fact** is not shown, since the acts never see what was withdrawn.

**Reading is shown the wrong Feedback on Facts from Items on its Topics.** *Wrong* on a **Fact** points at reading, and a piece of it is a precedent for reading an **Item** when the wrong **Fact**'s **Item** shares a **Topic** with it. **Topic** alone, because an **Item**'s **Entities** are known only once it is read, and its metadata (a title, a summary) names none apart from its text; matching **Entities** first would need a model call before the one that reads. Reading is shown at most eight, those sharing the most **Topics** first, then the newest, each with the wrong **Fact**, the quote it was drawn from and the **Depth** it was given at, after the **Item** and as data, like placing's. It is not told the **Depth** the **Item** is followed at now: whether a proposition is true, or is a **Claim** taken for a **Fact**, does not depend on how closely the **Owner** follows it, so the **Depth** says only how closely the **Owner** was reading when they said so. Only *wrong* is shown: *new to me* on a **Fact** says nothing about how it was drawn, and *missed* and *unwanted* point at placing. The wrong **Fact** is withdrawn, so this is the one place an act sees what was withdrawn, and it sees it only as what the **Editor** got wrong, never as part of the **Record**: the quote check runs against the **Item**'s text alone, so a **Fact** quoting a precedent does not trace and the reading is refused. The precedents shown are kept with the reading's result, as with placing. A precedent is a case, never a rule: reading's standard for what is a **Fact** and what a **Claim** is stated once, in its instructions, and what reading is told about precedents appears only when it is shown some, saying how to use them and restating no definition. A second statement of the standard, shown on every call, would shift the line between **Fact** and **Claim** on every **Item**, with or without **Feedback** behind it, which is editing the act's instructions from **Feedback** by another route (*Feedback fixes the Record and becomes precedent*). So an **Item** with no precedents is read exactly as it would be without them, and precedent changes only the readings it is about. If **Feedback** shows the standard itself is wrong, changing it is a deliberate change to the instructions, read on **Accuracy**.

**Feedback is kept as given, apart from how it is applied.** How **Feedback** is applied is expected to change as go-slow is developed. Keeping each piece as what the **Owner** said, about what, at what **Depth** and when, rather than only as its effect on the **Record**, lets a changed mechanism be run over all past **Feedback** instead of starting from none. Each piece's **Depth** is the closest **Depth** of the **Topics** tying the **Items** of the **Facts** it concerns, as it stands when the **Feedback** is given, since a **Topic** can later be deepened or dropped. Its effect is worked out from the piece alone, in plain code with no model call, and applied as marks on the **Record** that name the **Feedback** that made them; nothing is deleted. Deleting a wrong **Fact** would lose the trail from the **Feedback** to what it corrected and break every **Briefing** statement that cited it, and the **Record** is never rewritten (*A Fact holds as at a date*). The acts never see what was withdrawn, and the pages show it marked.

**Undoing Feedback is a mark, not a deletion.** **Feedback** given by mistake is undone from the page it shows on or the Feedback page. The piece stays kept as given and is marked undone, with when, so the trail from what the **Owner** said to what it changed survives the undo, and the Feedback page lists it marked. Deleting it would lose that trail, and with it why a past act was shown it as precedent (*precedents shown* name the piece). Undoing lifts the marks the piece made by working each one out again from the **Feedback** still standing: a mark names the earliest standing piece that makes it, or nothing. So a **Development** withdrawn by two *wrong* pieces on its **Fact** stays withdrawn until both are undone, without a mark having to name every piece that makes it: since each piece's effect is worked out from the piece alone, the standing **Feedback** is enough to say which marks hold, and a mark naming one piece is only the record of the first. What *missed* added stays in the **Record**, withdrawn by the undo, since **Briefing** statements and the **Sunday read** may cite it and the **Record** is never rewritten; it is shown nowhere, and the **Owner** can add it again. An undone piece is never precedent, since the **Owner** has said it does not hold, and the register does not count it (*Read from Feedback*). Undoing a piece already undone changes nothing. Editing a piece in place is not offered: the **Owner** undoes it and gives it again, so each piece stays what was said.

**Each kind of Feedback has the levels it can fix.** *Wrong* is about a proposition, so it is given on a **Fact** or a **Connection**: a **Story** is not true or false, and a **Development** that should not be one is *unwanted*, not wrong. *Wrong* on a **Fact** withdraws it with its **Developments** and its **Connections**, so the **Stories** connected only through it are no longer connected; *wrong* on a **Connection** withdraws only it. *Unwanted* is given on what the **Briefing** shows, a **Story** or a **Development**: an unwanted **Development** is out of its **Briefing**, and an unwanted **Story** out of every **Briefing**, the written ones and those to come, since steering which **Stories** the **Owner** is shown is what *unwanted* on a **Story** is for. *Missed* adds a **Story** (a title the **Owner** gives, gathering any of the **Facts** on the page as its **Developments**) or a **Development** (one of the **Facts** that share an **Entity** with the **Story**, the same neighbourhood placing weighs a **Fact** in). A missed **Fact** or **Connection** would have the **Owner** write it, doing the **Editor**'s work, so it is not offered. *New to me* is given on any of the four and changes nothing. The **Sunday read** takes **Feedback** at two more levels. An **Assessment** is a judgement, so it can be *wrong*, which withdraws it: the statements of any read that cite it are shown no more, and later reads are never shown it, nor an **Assessment** resting on a withdrawn **Fact**. The read as a whole takes *new to me*, which is what **Foresight** reads; it is not *wrong* or *unwanted* as a whole, since each statement is mended through what it cites. Neither is *missed*: a missed **Assessment** would have the **Owner** do the **Editor**'s reasoning.

**A written Briefing re-renders without what Feedback took out.** A **Briefing** is never rewritten, but it is shown without each statement that cites a **Development** withdrawn or unwanted, and without an unwanted **Story**. The statement is hidden rather than marked even when it cites other **Developments** too, because its prose may still assert the wrong **Fact**, and telling which words rest on which citation would need a model call. The **Story**'s page keeps the trail: it shows the withdrawn and unwanted **Developments**, marked, and the Feedback page lists every piece as given. Undoing the **Feedback** brings back what it hid. A written **Briefing** whose **Stories** **Feedback** took out entirely says so, and lists the **Feedback** that did, to undo, rather than saying nothing moved on: something did, and the **Owner** took it out, so a page indistinguishable from a quiet day would misread the **Record**.

**The Sunday read is the one place the Editor is an agent with tools.** It reads the week's **Stories**, then queries the **Record** (**Developments**, **Connections**, **Facts** and earlier **Assessments**, back through time) and the **Reference sources** as its argument needs them. Its questions differ each week and can't be fixed in advance, so a fixed sequence would be blind to what each read needs. Its quality is read through *new to me* **Feedback**, not by testing each step.

**The Sunday read writes Stories and Assessments, never Facts.** Building a wider story is creating a **Story** and gathering existing **Developments** into it. What the read works out, such as "Reform's slide in the polls began before the investigation", is kept as an **Assessment** citing the **Facts** it rests on. That is how go-slow threads its work over time: later reads start from earlier **Assessments** instead of from scratch, *placing a Fact* can see that a new **Fact** bears on one, and a **Connection** records a later **Fact** supporting or undercutting it. An **Assessment** is never rewritten; a later one supersedes it, so how go-slow's view moved stays readable. **Facts** come only from *reading an Item*, so they stay corroborated against what was collected; a read that wrote **Facts** would let an agent's reasoning enter the **Record** as if it had been collected. Every statement in the read cites a **Fact**, **Development**, **Assessment** or reference lookup, checked before the read is published.

**The read's citations are refs to what its agent was shown.** Each thing a tool shows comes with a ref (S3 a **Story**, F12 a **Fact**, S3:F12 a **Development**, A4 an **Assessment**, L1 a lookup of Wikidata made in this run, as the item and the revision seen), and the read is submitted through one tool, as statements each citing refs, the **Assessments** it reaches (N1, N2, each citing **Facts** and naming the current **Assessment** it supersedes, if any) and the **Stories** it starts (each gathering **Developments** by their S:F refs). The check in code refuses a submission with a statement that cites nothing, or anything the run was not shown and the read does not make, an **Assessment** citing no **Fact** shown, or superseding one not shown or already superseded, or a **Story** gathering a ref that is not a **Development**. Citing only what was shown, rather than anything in the **Record**, keeps an id the model recalls or guesses from passing as a citation. A refused submission is told back to the agent with the reason, so it can mend and resubmit within the run, since the work that found what to cite is in the conversation and starting again would lose it; nothing is published until a submission passes. The agent's tools only read: a **Story**'s whole **Timeline** with its cause **Connections** and connected **Stories**, the **Facts** involving an **Entity** back through time, earlier **Assessments**, and Wikidata's search and items. None writes, and the submission holds no **Facts**. What **Feedback** withdrew is never shown, as to every act.

**The read's loop is bounded, and written on Sunday at the Briefing's time.** The agent may take 40 turns and spend 4 million tokens (input, cached or not, and output), checked before each turn; a read of a dozen **Stories** with a few lookups each takes well under half of either, and a loop that never converges is stopped before it costs more than a few pounds. A turn that ends without submitting is asked once to submit, since a model can be asked to call a tool but not made to. With nothing the **Owner**'s **Topics** select moved on in the week, the read is written empty, with no model call. The read covers what moved on since the previous read was written (the first, the week before it), as the **Briefing** covers the day, and is written on Sunday just after the **Briefing**, at the time the **Owner** sets for it: one time is all *The outputs appear* needs, and Sunday's **Briefing** links the read. A failed read is kept and shown, and tried again an hour later, at most three times on the Sunday: it costs far more than a **Briefing**, and one that failed its checks or its bounds is likely to again.

## Stories

**Stories don't nest.** A wider story is a **Story** like any other, sharing **Developments** with narrower ones. Because one **Fact** can be a **Development** in several **Stories**, the ITV investigation's resignations sit in both *the ITV investigation into Reform* and *Reform's standing* without either containing the other. A hierarchy would break on the common case of a story belonging to two wider ones (that investigation is also part of *undercover journalism into parties*), so it would be a graph anyway, with a false tree implied. Building a wider story is starting a **Story** and gathering existing **Developments** into it, which is much of what the Sunday read does.

**Connections are made between Facts and derived between Stories.** The editorial work makes **Connections** from a **Fact** to another **Fact** (the investigation caused the resignations), and gives each **Entity** a **Fact** involves its role (ITV Exposure the actor, Reform UK the undergoer, in "ITV Exposure investigated Reform UK"). A role is part of what the **Fact** says, so it is not a **Connection**: a **Connection** is a tie between happenings or judgements that no single statement makes, and keeping the two apart is what every scheme for news content does (participants inside the event, links between events outside it; [news content research](../research/92-news-content-modelling.md)). Two **Stories** are connected when they share **Developments**, or when their **Facts** involve the same **Entities** in the same roles: that is how "other investigations into Reform" and "who carried out the investigation" are found. A **Connection** stored directly between **Stories** would hold its reason only in the editor's head and go stale as the **Stories** grow. A derived one always points at the shared **Facts** that justify it, and appears the moment placing makes a shared **Development** or connecting gives two **Facts** an **Entity** in the same role, with no act of its own. A **Story**'s page shows the **Stories** connected to it, each with its reasons: the shared **Developments**, and for each shared **Entity** and role the latest **Fact** on each side.

## Facts and Claims

**A Fact is corroborated against the primary Item where the Record holds one.** The text of a speech, a statutory instrument or a poll's tables outranks reports of them, and an original report outranks a pickup of it. A proposition the **Editor** can't corroborate stays a **Claim**, attributed to whoever made it. Because **Collection** never searches, a primary document is in the **Record** only if its publisher is a **Source**. That is a reason to choose primary publishers (GOV.UK, Parliament, pollsters) as **Sources** alongside the outlets that report on them.

**A Fact rests on its Item's own words.** *Reading an Item* gives every **Fact** and **Claim** a quote copied from the **Item**, and its check refuses the whole result if any **Fact**'s quote is not in the **Item**'s text (compared as words, so punctuation, curly quotes and markup don't matter). The **Item** a **Fact** carries is set by the act, not by the model, so it can't be wrong; what can be wrong is the **Fact** itself, a proposition the model brought in from its own knowledge rather than drew from the **Item**, which is exactly the untraced statement *Everything traces* guards against. A quote makes that checkable in plain code on every reading, where judging whether a paraphrase follows from a text would need another model call. A **Claim** must also name who made it, or the result is refused: a **Claim** with no attribution is a **Fact** in disguise. A refused result writes nothing, and its **Item** waits to be read again on the next **Collection** run, rather than keeping the parts that passed, which would leave an **Item** half-read with nothing to show what was dropped.

**A Fact holds as at a date.** What was true when it was recorded may not be true now (a "former" leader, a PM since a given day). History in the **Record** stays true of its date and is never rewritten to the present. So what something will do, cause or be worth (an estimate, a forecast, a projected effect: "the headquarters will give the region's economy a £151 million boost") is never a **Fact**: as at the **Item**'s date nothing has happened that could corroborate it, and no source or effort can, which is what makes it a **Claim**, attributed and never proved. *Reading an Item* draws it as a **Claim** made by whoever the **Item** attributes it to, or by the **Item**'s publisher, as the **Item** presents itself, when it attributes it to no one; a projection drawn as a **Fact** would let the **Briefing** state as having happened what is only expected. Reading's input shows the **Item**'s title, date and text, not the **Source** it came from, so the publisher is named from the **Item** itself.

**An arrangement is a Fact; the event it arranges is not yet.** When an **Item** reports that a future event has been arranged ("I can confirm to the House today that it will take place in Brussels on the 20th November"), the **Fact** is the arrangement as at the **Item**'s date, worded as a present state of affairs ("the UK-EU summit is scheduled for Brussels on 20 November"), never as the event ("will take place"). The arrangement can be corroborated now (the other party's announcement, an official calendar), so it is not a projection; the event becomes a **Fact** only when an **Item** reports that it happened, and an arrangement later changed stays true of its date, as a calendar entry moved to another date does (*Releases are collected; series are consulted*). Drawing it as a **Claim** would make it unable to be a **Development**, so a summit being fixed could never move a **Story** on; drawing only the saying ("the PM confirmed that…") would hand the **Briefing** an attribution to restate where the **Item** reports a state of affairs.

**A declared intention is a Fact, as an intention.** When an **Item** reports that someone has said what they will do ("the UK will bring further sanctions to restrict the capacity of Iran and the IRGC to do the UK harm"), the **Fact** is that they intend it, worded as the intention ("the UK intends to bring further sanctions against Iran and the IRGC"), never as the act done ("will bring", "brings"), which nothing can yet corroborate. An announced intention is news in itself, and it is taken at its word, whoever announces it: what reaches the news has already been through the news's own filter, and go-slow does not rank **Sources** or speakers by trustworthiness, so a qualifier on who may be believed (whether they have the power to carry it out) would be that ranking under another name. Drawn as a **Claim**, an intention could never be a **Development**, and a government announcing new sanctions could never move a **Story** on. What stays a **Claim** is what something will do, cause or be worth beyond the intention itself: "the deals will add over £10 billion a year to the UK economy by 2040" is the speaker's estimate, not their intention, and is a **Claim** by *A Fact holds as at a date*. Whether an intention moves a **Story** on is placing's judgement, not reading's: one that restates a stance already held ("we will continue to stand with Saudi Arabia against the Houthis") is background, unless it departs from the **Story**'s **Timeline**. Drawing what was said never replaces that it was said, nor what it answers: when an **Item** reports someone saying or responding to something, the saying and the event it responds to are **Facts** too, beside the **Facts** and **Claims** drawn from what was said. Otherwise a reaction's content crowds out the news it reacts to: a government's response to an agreement between two other states reads as a list of its stated positions (it intends to maintain sanctions, it remains committed to Ukraine), and the **Record** holds neither the agreement, a **Development** in its own right, nor that the government responded, which is what placing needs to judge that a reaction moves no **Story** on.

## How material arrives

**News material arrives only through collection.** Collection checks a fixed set of known sources on its own timetable, separate from the briefings', and lands what it finds in the record. The daily briefing and the Sunday read both work over that record; neither fetches news material of its own.

One way in keeps every request to a news source on one timetable, where politeness (request rates, conditional requests, robots.txt) is enforced in one place. A Sunday read that fetched topic-led material while it was being written would be a second, irregular visitor to the same outlets, with its own politeness to get right, and would turn collection's "check the same known sources" into topic search.

**Collection is polite by these rules.** A publisher's robots.txt is necessary but not sufficient: several allow a generic bot and forbid automated access in their terms, so a **Source** is admitted on its terms, not its robots.txt. **Collection** reads feeds and APIs, not pages. It makes conditional requests where the server supports them and otherwise polls no faster than the feed's own `ttl` or `Cache-Control`. It runs one request at a time per host, backs off the whole host on 429 or 503 for as long as `Retry-After` says, and fetches each robots.txt at most once a day. Each **Source** also has its own rate limit, the least time between two fetches of it, so an API that can't answer conditionally is asked no more often than it changes. Hosts that block scripted clients (Parliament's main site and Hansard) are never fetched, whatever their robots.txt says; their publisher is read only through its APIs. It identifies itself honestly, with a fixed User-Agent naming go-slow and a contact address, and never imitates a browser: a disguised client takes away the publisher's means of addressing it, and some terms treat circumvention as a breach. The findings behind these rules are in [sources research](../research/2-sources.md).

**The record keeps everything collected.** Every item collection brings in stays in the record, including the most that warrant nothing, and the editorial work is layered over it. Whether a detail matters depends on how closely the owner follows its topic, and that changes: something passed over today becomes worth linking to once the owner follows its topic closely. With no backfill, an item dropped when it warranted nothing could never be recovered. How much of each **Item** is kept is set per **Source** by its terms, and a publisher becomes a **Source** only if its terms let the **Record** keep what the **Editor** needs: full text under an open licence (GOV.UK and Parliament under the Open Government and Open Parliament Licences), less where the terms allow less (a pollster's results with attribution). **Items** keep the personal data they carry (names in Hansard, in releases, in polls) as published. Having been published elsewhere does not stop a name being personal data; what puts the **Record** outside UK GDPR is that go-slow is private, a purely personal activity (an exemption taken from recall, not yet checked against sources). Opening go-slow to other readers would bring it under UK GDPR as well as the licences.

**An Item is collected with its full text, or not yet.** Where a **Source**'s feed carries only a summary or an opening (GOV.UK's Atom entries, Parliament's written statements list), **Collection** fetches each new entry's full text from the publisher's API (GOV.UK's Content API, the written statement itself), by the same politeness rules, and keeps the **Item** only once it has it. Every **Item** gets its text, not only those tied to a **Topic**, because the check ties by terms in an **Item**'s text: fetching text only after a tie would leave the check matching summaries, and miss exactly the **Items** a **Topic** taken up later should find. An entry whose text can't be fetched on this run is left out of it and collected on the next, while it is still in the feed, so an **Item** is always whole and nothing downstream (the check, reading, the pages) needs a state for one awaiting its text. *Reading an Item* is shown its text without its summary, which stands in only for an **Item** with no text: the summary is the publisher's description of the text, not more of it, and shown beside it, **Facts** get drawn from the description (a speech's "delivered a speech in Scotland today") rather than from what the speech says; the quote check runs against the same input, so a **Fact** quoting only the summary of an **Item** with text does not trace. Collecting the entry at once and completing it later would never miss one, but adds that state and its retries for a case only a long outage at one host makes real: an entry stays in a feed of 50 for a day or more, and a host down that long shows its **Source** quiet (*No Source goes quiet*). **Items** collected before their text was fetched are given it once, by the `backfill` admin command, only while they are unread: nothing has been drawn from an unread **Item**, so giving it its text changes no **Fact**, and the check is run over it again. An **Item** already read stays as it was read, since its **Facts** hold as at that reading, and reading it again would draw the same **Facts** twice.

**Sources are primary-led.** The **Sources** are primary publishers (government departments, Parliament, statistics bodies, pollsters, parties' own releases) and any reporting outlet whose terms permit storing and summarising its **Items**. Outlets whose terms forbid that are not collected at all: no **Items**, no headlines, no links. The major UK outlets forbid it: the BBC names summaries for one's own use and agentic AI, the Guardian bans automated reading and any AI use, and Reuters requires written consent. Being private does not change this, because UK law permits the temporary copies made while reading but not the stored copies the **Record** keeps. Keeping headline-and-link pointers to those outlets would still take the metadata the BBC's terms forbid taking. The cost is the stories that exist only as reporting, such as an undercover investigation: they reach go-slow through their consequences (resignations, statements, notices), not through the reporting itself. Building from primary material is also what makes corroboration against the primary **Item** the norm rather than the exception.

**The owner chooses the sources; topics don't.** The known sources are a list the owner maintains by hand. Topics of interest act only in the editorial work, on what collection brings in. Following a new topic may lead the owner to add a source, but nothing derives sources from topics: doing so would need source discovery from a topic, which is topic search under another name, and would let the list grow beyond what can be visited politely.

**Releases are collected; series are consulted.** A statistics body or central bank publishes two different things. A release (the ONS's GDP bulletin, an OBR forecast, the MPC's statement) is published on a date, says something and makes news: it is an **Item**, collected on the timetable, and the bodies' release calendars are **Sources** of scheduled events. A calendar entry is kept as an **Item** with the date it is due and is upcoming until then, so the **Editor** can see a release coming before it says anything; the release itself arrives later as its own **Item**. An entry moved to another date is a new **Item**, since the **Record** is never rewritten, and only its latest date is upcoming. A series (GDP by quarter, the 10-year gilt yield, sterling against the dollar) is background: it is a **Reference source**, looked up when a release or event makes it matter ("yields rose after the Budget"). A daily yield is not news, and collecting series would fill the **Record** with numbers that warrant nothing. It also keeps market data within what is openly licensed (official yield curves, reference exchange rates, policy rates), since commercial market data is licensed and not to be stored.

**Reference sources are consulted, not collected.** Wikidata, historic datasets (past polls, election results) and statistical series hold background facts about the world rather than news. The editorial work looks things up in them when it needs that background, for instance the Sunday read placing a week's poll against months of earlier ones.

**The record starts empty and is never backfilled.** Collection's first run sees only what the known sources carry that day, and the record deepens week by week. Early Sunday reads therefore have the long-run facts, from reference sources, but no earlier news: an investigation from last year is not in the record until something brings it back. Backfilling from the sources' archives would close that gap, but it is a heavy one-off visit to every outlet, the kind of fetch politeness rules out, and it would leave months of material with no editorial work done on it. The gap closes on its own as the record grows.
