# System design

The design of go-slow: how it models the editorial work of a slow-news briefing, and why it has this shape. Terms are defined in [`CONTEXT.md`](../../CONTEXT.md). Hypotheses are stated here without status; status is in the register, derived from readings, and until the first reading every hypothesis is Due.

## End state

**The Owner keeps reading the Briefing and the Sunday read.** Continued use is the top because the mechanism of go-slow's value is hard to pin down: whatever it is, a reader who keeps coming back is getting it, and a reader who stops is not. go-slow sits on top of the outlets it collects from, not in place of them, so the test is use, not displacement of other news. The parts below are the faster, finer readings that say why use holds or falls.

*Reading rule.* Over a rolling 8-week window, count every day a **Briefing** is opened and every **Sunday read** opened.
- **Refuted**, in any window: fewer than 2 days in 7 on average, or 3 or fewer of the 8 **Sunday reads**. Either output being abandoned refutes it, since both are the end state.
- **Supported**, only in a quiet window (commits to go-slow on no more than 1 day in 7 on average): at least 4 days in 7, and at least 6 of the 8 **Sunday reads**.
- **Undecided**: anything else.

The **Owner** is also the builder, and opens the outputs to check work as well as to read them. That error inflates use, so a busy window can refute but never support: if even the builder doesn't open the **Briefing**, that is strong evidence; if the builder does, it is weak. Separating reading from checking by excluding days with commits fails while development is daily, because it excludes nearly every day. An undecided window is re-read on the next fresh window, judged alone. Review every 8 weeks once the **Briefing** runs daily.

### Necessary conditions

Three cheap, coarse conditions for any use at all. Each runs on every change and on the running system; any failure refutes, and passing confirms nothing. Without them a broken **Collection** or a missing **Briefing** would surface weeks later, as a dip in use.

- **The outputs appear.** A **Briefing** every day and a **Sunday read** every Sunday, by a time the **Owner** sets. Refuted by any missed day.
- **Everything traces.** Every statement of a **Briefing** or a **Sunday read**, and every **Assessment**, cites at least one **Span** in the **Record**, an **Assessment** or a lookup of a **Reference source**. Refuted by any that cites none. Statements with nothing behind them are the way AI news summaries fail in public (see [prior art](../research/5-prior-art.md)), and this is the mechanical guard against it.
- **No Source goes quiet.** Every **Source** yields new **Items** within its usual gap. Refuted by any **Source** silent past it without notice. The usual gap is declared with the **Source** when it is added, and silence runs from its newest **Item**'s publication, or from when collecting it began if it has yielded none, so a **Source** that never yields is caught too. Counting from publication rather than from when go-slow last collected means a **Collection** that has stopped running shows every **Source** quiet, which is the notice wanted. The Items page shows the flag.

### Read from Feedback

The four parts below are all read from one instrument, the **Feedback** in the **Record**, by its kind. The **Owner** gives **Feedback** in the course of reading and building anyway, so these readings cost nothing extra and carry the signal while the top can't be supported. They need no ground truth of what mattered: an outside record (a year-in-review page, an outlet's weekly roundup) is sparse, one outlet's judgement, or not licensed to keep, and the **Owner**'s **Feedback** is the only standard of what matters here anyway. Each count is broken down by what it was given on (the **Judgement**'s act for *wrong*; the **Briefing**'s **Spans** for *unwanted* and *missed*), so a part that moves shows where. **Feedback** the **Owner** undid is not counted: it was a mistake in giving, and counting it would read the **Owner**'s slip as the **Editor**'s.

*Shared reading rule for the three parts that should fall.* Count **Feedback** of the part's kind per **Briefing**, from the **Record** and never by hand, over the last 4 weeks against the 4 before. **Refuted** if the rate rose; **supported** if it fell by at least a quarter; **undecided** otherwise. A fall also results from the **Owner** giving less **Feedback** at all, so these are read only while the top is not refuted. Both windows lie wholly after the **Record** became events with the **Span** as its unit: each kind of **Feedback** lands on different things either side of it (*wrong* on a **Judgement**, not a **Fact**; *missed* on a **Span**, not a **Story**), so a rate compared across it would measure the change of instrument as much as the **Editor**. Whether that change made things worse is read by the top, which reads straight through it. Due every 4 weeks, and on any change to the **Editor**, including a new **Judge** and a change to how **Feedback** is applied.

### Coverage: the Editor misses less

*Missed* **Feedback** falls: the **Spans** the **Owner** has to point out as worth telling become fewer.

### Brevity: the Editor includes less that isn't wanted

*Unwanted* **Feedback** falls: the **Owner** cuts less. Brevity is what the **Owner** stops cutting, not a reading-time budget, because a budget would cut what matters on a heavy day and pad a light one.

### Accuracy: the Editor gets less wrong

*Wrong* **Feedback** falls: fewer mistaken **Judgements** (a reference filled in wrongly, a speaker misjudged, a *follows* that doesn't, a statement that misstates its **Spans**). Because the **Record** feeds the editorial work, accuracy is what keeps the other parts from degrading as the **Record** grows.

### Foresight: the Editor shows the Owner what they hadn't seen

*New to me* **Feedback** keeps coming, mostly on **Stories** and on the **Sunday read**. This is where go-slow differs from existing news tools, none of which follows a **Story** as an ongoing subject or connects subjects by cause (see [prior art](../research/5-prior-art.md)).

*Reading rule.* Over each 4-week window, **refuted** if there is no *new to me* **Feedback**; **supported** if there is some in at least 3 of the 4 **Sunday reads**; **undecided** otherwise. Read only while the top is not refuted. Due every 4 weeks.

## Shape

The editorial work is the one core domain. Collection and storage are adapters around it (the Web apps Guide's ports and adapters): they fetch and keep material, and make no editorial judgement. The value of the project is the editorial work, so it is the part kept free of I/O and tested directly.

**An agent edits, and the Owner steers.** The **Editor** does the editorial work every day without waiting on the **Owner**, guided by the **Owner**'s **Feedback**. The **Owner** reads its output and gives **Feedback** on it, and the **Feedback** is kept in the **Record**. Steering is **Feedback** too: telling the **Editor** what was missed or unwanted is how the **Owner** influences what they are shown. A briefing the **Owner** had to edit would wait on the **Owner**'s time, the opposite of one they read. An **Editor** with no **Feedback** loop would let its mistakes compound, because the **Record** feeds the editorial work: later **Judgements** are made over earlier ones, so a wrong *refers to* left standing puts the wrong **Entity** behind every candidate found through it. Its form is under *The Editor*.

**The editorial output is the same for every reader.** The **Owner**'s **Feedback** shapes the editorial work, and everyone who reads the briefing reads the same one. There is no model of an audience: no per-reader selection, no segments, no engagement signal. A briefing personalised per reader would multiply the editorial work by the number of readers and turn judgement into ranking. One output keeps the editorial work the thing that is done, once.

**go-slow is private: the Owner is its only reader.** It is not publicly reachable. Publishers' terms and UK copyright law draw their sharpest line between private, personal, non-commercial use and publishing to others, so a private go-slow can use the widest set of **Sources** and keep the most of each. Opening it to other readers changes nothing in the editorial design, since the output is already the same for every reader, but it changes what the **Record** may hold and which **Sources** may be collected, and is made only once licensing allows it.

**go-slow runs when the Owner starts it.** It runs on the **Owner**'s laptop, from the same image and compose file it is developed with, and its port is published on the loopback interface only, so it stays private on any network the laptop joins: a port open on every interface would serve the **Record** to everyone else on a shared network. The **Record** is an ordinary folder on the laptop, mounted into the container, so the **Owner** can see it, copy it and keep it across resets of Docker. Nothing keeps go-slow running when the **Owner** hasn't started it, because starting it is reading it: the **Owner** starts it each day to read, and a host running it unattended would write **Briefings** nobody opens, which nothing in the end state counts. A day it isn't started is a day not read, and the top's reading already counts it as one, so it needs no rule of its own. Under *The outputs appear* as it stands, a day it isn't started has no **Briefing** by the set time and is a missed day, and so is a day it is started after the set time; whether that condition should count only the days go-slow ran is the **Owner**'s review, and until then its rule stands.

## Record construction

**The Span is the unit, and it is an address.** A **Span** is a stretch of an **Item**'s text, known by where it starts and ends, and everything go-slow says about what an **Item** says is a judgement on its **Spans**. A **Span** is never reworded: a proposition reworded from its **Item** stutters against the text it came from, and in doing so it resolves references ("the city" becomes Derby), which is a judgement of its own, invents attributions ("Andy Burnham said:" becomes the subject of every sentence after it), and loses the speaker's voice ("This is fantastic news for Derby", the Mayor's view, restated in go-slow's own). A **Span** keeps all three apart: the words are the publisher's, and what they refer to and who speaks them are judgements on them. Because the text is never rewritten, an address always points at the same words, so no judgement, **Briefing** statement or **Feedback** is ever left pointing at something changed, and *Everything traces* is a property of the address rather than a check on a quote. The whole **Item** is not the unit either: an **Item** says several things that belong to different **Stories** (a press release moves devolution on and repeats a line about centralisation from the speech), and a relation between whole **Items** would tie everything in one to everything in the other.

**The grain belongs to the judgement, not to the Span.** **Spans** nest, from the whole **Item** to a word, and each judgement is about the stretch it needs: *refers to* is about "the city" and "Derby", *follows* about a clause, a **Briefing** statement about the sentence it rests on, *wrong* about the three words that are. A **Cut** lists an **Item**'s **Spans** before anything else is judged of them (sentences, today, by plain code), so that each judge is shown the **Item** as numbered **Spans** and cites them by number, which models do reliably, where character offsets they do not; what is stored is the address the number stands for, and a judge can cite a finer stretch as words quoted within a **Span** of the **Cut**. A fixed grain chosen at construction would make every later judgement work at it, and would need a judgement of its own about which **Spans** the **Briefing** consumes. The **Cut** is a judgement like the others, kept with its cutter, so a better cutter (one per **Source**, or a model) adds a **Cut** without invalidating any judgement made on an earlier one: a judgement is about an address, whichever **Cut** listed it.

**Construction judges from one Item; editorial work judges over the Record at a time.** The **Editor**'s work falls into two subsystems by what a judgement reads. *Record construction* judges from a single **Item** (its **Cut**, what its **Spans** refer to and name, who speaks them, which are not useful), so it runs as each **Item** arrives, independently of every other. *Editorial work* judges over the **Record** as it stands at a time (*follows*, *same as*, *contradicts*, what the **Briefing** tells), and can run whenever it is wanted as well as on a schedule. Hydration is construction, though it is a judgement and needs a model, because it reads only the **Item**; and it has to be, because a **Span** that names its subject only as "the city" mentions Derby only once *refers to* is judged, so leaving hydration until editorial work asked for it would keep that **Span** out of every candidate found through Derby.

**Reading an Item makes construction's model judgements in one call.** Shown the **Item** as its **Cut**, *reading an Item* judges what its **Spans** *refer to*, what they name (a name and its kind), who speaks them, and which are **Rejected** as not useful. Each judgement is kept as its own, with the call as its judge, so a view can take one kind from a newer judge and another from an older one. They are made together because they depend on each other (the speaker of "This is fantastic news for the city" is the Mayor through "he"; whether a **Span** says anything depends on what it refers to), and four acts judging them apart could contradict each other, would send the same **Item** four times and would wait on four batches before a **Span** was hydrated.

**Entities are looked up by name, not by Span.** What a name refers to in Wikidata is looked up in plain code, keyed on the name and its kind, and for a person named by office on the office and the **Item**'s date, so "Derby" in a hundred **Spans** is one look-up, and a look-up refused or delayed holds up no reading. A look-up made per reading would repeat the same request for every **Item** naming a common **Entity**, at the one request a second Wikidata's rules allow, and one refusal would stop the reading that made it.

## The Record as events

**The Record keeps what was observed or judged; everything else is a view.** An **Item** collected, a **Cut**, each judgement (a *refers to*, a name, a speaker, a **Rejected**, a *follows*, *same as* or *contradicts*, a **Briefing** with the **Stories** it gathered and the relations it noticed while writing), each piece of **Feedback** and each undoing of one is kept as an event, at its own grain, with an identifier and the version of its shape, and is never changed. What can be worked out again from them is a view: a **Story**, corroboration, which **Spans** mention an **Entity**, what a page shows, the register. The **Record** as it stood at a time recurs through the design: editorial work runs over it, **Feedback** is point in time, and a view chooses whose judgements it reads, and a **Record** that kept only its current state could answer none of these. go-slow leaned this way before it was event-based: **Feedback**'s effects were worked out from the piece alone so a changed mechanism could be re-run, undoing was a mark, kept calls were never rewritten, and the register was derived on every view. **Sources** and **Topics** are kept as plain records, changed in place: they are configuration the **Owner** sets, not something observed or judged. Shapes are versioned because events are never rewritten: a view reads every version of an event it has ever been given, rather than a migration rewriting history into the newest shape.

**Text is kept apart from the events, so it can be forgotten.** An **Item**'s text is stored beside the events, which refer to it by **Item** and address, so a **Source**'s terms that require forgetting an **Item** are met by removing its text, while the events stay append-only and their addresses point into text that is gone. Forgetting has to reach every copy: the kept calls and the recordings hold text too. Every **Source** today is under the Open Government or Open Parliament Licence with full text kept, so nothing is forgotten yet. An address is into the text as kept, the publisher's HTML, not into a plain-text rendering of it, since a rendering is code that can change and would move every address with it; the words at an address are read by dropping its tags. An **Item** collected with only its summary is not cut until its text is fetched, so no **Cut** addresses text that changes after it.

**Every judgement carries its judge, and a changed judge judges forward.** A judge is the act, its instructions' version, the model and the request's hash, as kept calls record. A new version of an act is a new judge: it judges what arrives from then on, and the judgements made before stand, with their judge. A view reads, for each thing judged, the newest judge that has judged it, so the **Record** mixes judges over time, and every page and the register can say which judge made what they show. Re-judging the whole **Record** with every change would cost as much as the **Record** is large, growing daily, for every instruction tried; re-judging a range is a deliberate run on the **Owner**'s say, priced first, as a re-recording is. A candidate judge is compared on the live **Record** by re-judging a sample, kept beside the live judgements and read by no view until it is chosen; since **Spans** are addresses that never change, no judgement or **Feedback** is ever left pointing at nothing by a judge that judged differently. Choosing a candidate is itself an event, and rewrites none of its judgements: from the choice on, the candidate is a live judge whose judgements count as made when it was chosen, so they are newer than every live judgement made before the choice, and a live judgement made after it is newer still. Ordering them by when they were made instead would let a live judge that judged the same thing between the candidate's run and its choice outrank the judge just chosen. A candidate is chosen only on the model and version the act runs on from then, so the judge the **Owner** chose is the one that goes on judging what arrives. A re-judging run calls the model in real time, one request at a time, though a batch costs half: the run is a one-off the **Owner** starts and watches end, and a batch would either hold it open for up to a day or leave its results to the app's timetable, which applies only work awaiting an act, not work judged again. Its price is estimated before it runs, in dollars at real-time rates, from a price table kept with the model adapter and dated, and the dry run prints the date, so a stale table shows where it is used; the run stops before a call that could take it over the most it may spend, since the estimate is only an estimate.

## Editorial work

**Relating a Span judges follows, same as and contradicts in one call, once per pair.** Once an **Item** is read, each of its **Spans** is shown candidate **Spans** of other **Items** already read, and judges each as one it *follows*, the *same as* it, one it *contradicts*, or none of these. The three are judged together because they are three answers to one comparison of one pair. Each pair is judged once, when the second of the two is read: a **Span** is never judged against what arrives after it, since that arrival judges the pair. "Already read" is the trigger, not the order of publication: an **Item** published on Monday and collected on Wednesday is related to Tuesday's, which was read before it, and *follows* still runs from the later-published **Span** to the earlier, whichever arrived first; pairing by publication alone would never judge a pair whose earlier **Item** arrived second. **Spans** of one **Item** are not related to each other: they are already tied by being announced together, and *follows* within an **Item** would be the order of its sentences. Relating creates nothing, only relations between **Spans** that are already in the **Record**, so a batch needs no ordering: two **Items** in one batch are both read before either is related, and the pair is judged once, by the later-published, which is read second because a batch's readings are applied in the order their **Items** were published. An **Item** is related once what its reading names has been looked up, since the **Entities** are what find its candidates, and its **Spans** are weighed together against every candidate any of them found: a reading names a thing where the **Item** names it first, not every time it recurs ("Scotland" once in a speech that says it on every page), so a **Span** whose own names find nothing is still weighed against what its neighbours found. An **Item** whose **Spans** find no candidates is related with no call. *Same as* across independent **Sources** is corroboration, worked out from the relations rather than judged again; *same as* within one publisher (a speech and its press release) is not.

**A Story is a view from a Span, gathered at a time.** A **Span**'s story is the **Spans** it moves on from and those that move it on, gathered by a judge shown the **Span** and the **Spans** its relations reach, who keeps what moves on what this **Span** says. Its subject is what the **Span** says, so it needs no judgement of its own about what it is about. *Follows* is a judgement about a pair, and says that one **Span** moves another on but not on what: a **Span** saying two things (a headquarters' site and the jobs it brings) is followed on each by different **Spans**, so taking a **Story** to be whatever *follows* ties together would join both subjects through it, and over months would join most of the **Record** into one. Taking a **Story** to be a root and all that follows from it only slows the same drift, since each step can be fair and the chain still wander. A **Story** stored as a thing **Spans** join would hold its subject, but every **Span** would have to be judged into one, and its identity would change as it merged and split, under the **Feedback** given on it. Seen from a **Span**, the viewpoint supplies the subject, and the judge sees the whole and drops what drifted. What this costs is memory: nothing holds a **Story** between two gatherings, so two days' **Briefings** may gather a subject differently. **Feedback** is point in time, so it needs no **Story** that stays the same to point at.

**A Story is gathered by the pass that tells it.** Gathering is a judgement, a model call over the whole, so it is made inside the editorial passes that tell a **Story** (the **Briefing**, for each **Span** it tells; the **Sunday read**, for its own) and kept with that pass's judgement, with its judge and time. A **Span** told that relates to nothing has an empty **Story**, kept without asking the judge, since nothing could be in it. A **Span**'s page shows its latest gathering, then the relations judged since, marked as not yet gathered, and never calls a model, so what it shows is always a judgement someone made, dated. Gathering every **Span**'s story as it arrives would be a second call per **Span**, when every **Span** is read and related and most are never told; a **Span** no pass has told has only its relations, and its story matters once something tells it.

## The Editor

**The Editor's daily work is a sequence of editorial acts.** Each act is a bounded step with a typed result, reading a slice of the **Record** and writing back only what passes its checks:

1. *Cutting an Item* lists its **Spans**, in plain code.
2. *Reading an Item* judges what its **Spans** refer to, what they name, who speaks them and which are **Rejected**; plain code then looks the names up in Wikidata.
3. *Relating a Span* judges what it *follows*, is the *same as* and *contradicts*.
4. *Writing the Briefing* judges what to tell of the **Spans** read since the last, gathers their **Stories** and writes statements citing them.

An act is a model call where judgement is needed and plain code where it is not. A single agent with tools over the **Record**, deciding for itself what to do each day, fails on four counts. **Feedback** lands on a judgement, and so on the act that made it (*Feedback lands on a judgement*), so when a part of the end state worsens the act to fix is known; with one agent, only that the agent got worse. "Everything traces" becomes a property of each act's result (a judgement is about addresses in its **Item**) rather than something an agent must remember. Each act can be tested first, on recorded **Items**, by properties such as "a quoted **Span**'s speaker is who the **Item** says spoke it" and "a speech *follows* the press release announcing it". And the work grows with the number of **Items**, not with how far an agent chooses to explore.

**Every Item is read, and Topics select nothing.** Every **Item** collected is cut, read and related, whatever it is about. What the **Owner** reads is the **Editor**'s judgement (*The Briefing tells what the Editor judges worth telling*), and it leans to discovery: a **Story** need not touch a **Topic** to be worth telling, and a gate on **Topics** would hide exactly what the **Owner** didn't know to follow. Reading everything is cheap enough not to plan around at go-slow's volume, and keeping selection out of construction means a **Span** is read the same way whatever anyone follows, so no **Item** waits on interest that changes. The **Owner** steers instead through the **Sources**: what is shown per **Source** is how many of the **Stories** it contributes to touch a **Topic** and how many don't, and how many of its **Spans** are **Rejected**, and the **Owner** prunes **Sources** on it. A **Span** touches a **Topic** by the **Topic**'s terms, matched as whole words in the text a reader sees, with no model; that counts, and selects nothing. There is no **Depth**: how closely a **Topic** is followed decided what was read and told, and both are now the **Editor**'s judgement, guided by **Feedback**.

**The model is an adapter, and the one adapter is Claude.** Every act that calls a model does so through a provider-neutral port, and each act owns its instructions and output schema, so the acts are tested against a fake and each act's model is a configuration value. The one adapter is Claude through Anthropic's API, and which Claude model runs which act is chosen on the recorded fixtures by their properties ([model research](../research/models.md)). Open-weight models are not used. From an inference provider they are paid per token like Claude, and at go-slow's volume (about 100 daily calls) every candidate costs a few dollars a month, so the most they could save is less than a second adapter, a second set of recordings for every act and a measurement between providers cost to build and keep. Where they would be free at the margin, a model the **Owner** runs, they need a machine running all day, and the models such a machine holds are not the frontier ones. The port stays provider-neutral, so a second adapter stays a new integration rather than a redesign, if the cost of the calls ever outgrows the cost of the work. Each act runs on a pinned model ID, one whose weights the provider never changes, so behaviour can't change between two readings, or under a green recording, without a change in go-slow; an alias the provider moves to a newer model would change the act while every recording still passed. A model's ID is part of the act's request, so changing it is a re-record, like changing the act's instructions. Each result keeps the model the provider reports answering with, not the one asked for, so if a configured ID ever did move, the **Record** would show it.

**A provider never trains on go-slow's requests and keeps them only for a limited, stated period.** Most of what is sent is public, but requests carry the **Owner**'s **Feedback** as precedent, which together is a profile of what the **Owner** follows. Strict zero retention would shut out most closed models' standard terms for little gain over a profile of interests; a provider that trains on requests or keeps them indefinitely is excluded.

**The daily acts run in batches; the Briefing is written in real time.** Nothing waits on a single **Item**, and the design already accepts up to a **Collection** run between an **Item** arriving and its **Spans** being related, so the daily acts are submitted together and processed asynchronously, which is cheaper where a provider discounts batches, as Anthropic does. Writing the **Briefing** is one request just before it is due, so it runs at once. A batch is never waited on: after each **Collection** run the results of batches that have ended are applied, through each act's own checks, and then everything awaiting an act and not already in a batch is submitted as one batch. Results are matched to their work by the identifier each request was given, never by position, since a provider returns them in any order. Work stays awaiting its act until its result is written, and the pending batches are kept in the **Record**, so a restart picks them up and a result is applied to the work as it stands when it arrives. A result for work that would now be asked otherwise (its candidates have moved since the request was sent) answers a question no longer the work's, so it is refused and the work asked again as it stands. A request that failed, expired or fails its act's checks is resubmitted alone, so one bad **Item** confines its failure to itself, as *Construction and relating run as Items arrive* requires, rather than waiting for the next batch or holding one up.

**The Briefing doesn't wait for construction.** Nothing is collected while go-slow is stopped, so a morning start collects everything since the last run, and its reading and relating go out in batches that can take hours to end. The **Briefing** is written at the set time over what the **Record** holds then, and never waits on them: a **Span** is there for editorial work once its **Item** is cut, which is plain code done as the **Item** is collected, so the overnight **Items** are in the morning's **Briefing** whether or not their reading and relating have ended. A **Span** told before it is read or related is told with less (no hydration, no *follows* to say what it moves on), and that is accepted: the **Owner** prefers a **Briefing** on time over a better one late, and what is judged later shows on the **Span**'s page. Waiting for the start's batches is what made a **Briefing** miss its time, and a bound on the wait only chooses how late. On Sundays the **Sunday read** follows the **Briefing** as always.

**Work that keeps failing is given up after three attempts, and taken up again by hand.** Each piece of work (one act on one **Item**) gets three attempts across its batches, the first and each resubmission alike; after the third failure it is marked failed in the **Record**, with when and why it last failed, and is no longer submitted. A batch that has not ended, or cannot be polled, two days after it was submitted (twice the day within which a provider ends one) is given up, and counts a failed attempt for each piece of work in it, so a batch that disappears at the provider or never ends doesn't leave its work pending for good. A result that came back but fails to be applied for a reason other than the act's checks (a Wikidata lookup refused, the **Record** failing) is not an attempt: the attempt is the model call, and that call answered. It stops only that result: the batch's other results are applied in the same run, the batch is kept holding only the requests still to apply, so their work is not submitted again, and each run applies them again from the kept results, at no model call, until they apply. The line between waiting and counting is the batch's deadline: a result still failing to apply when its batch is two days old counts a failed attempt, as a lost batch's work does, so a fault that never passes ends in the work being marked failed and listed, not waiting for good. Stopping the whole run at such a result would hold up every result behind it and everything new waiting to be submitted, on every run, for one passing refusal. The cap is there because every attempt is a model call: a request whose answer always fails its checks, or is always refused, would otherwise be paid for on every **Collection** run without bound, and a failure that persists across three attempts, minutes to hours apart, is one in the **Item** or the act, not in the provider, so a fourth attempt is unlikely to fare better. Failed work no longer awaits its act, so nothing waits behind it. Failed work is listed on the **Items** page with its reason rather than dropped silently, since an **Item** never read or related is a gap in the **Record** the **Owner** should know of. Retrying is by hand, through the `retry` admin command (one act's failed work, or all of it), which clears the failed marks and resets the attempts: what fixes failed work is a change the system can't see (an act's instructions or checks, a provider's outage, a malformed **Item** corrected at its **Source**), so retrying on a timer would only spend the calls the cap saves, and the **Owner** is the one who knows the cause has been dealt with.

**Every model call is kept, as it was sent and as it came back.** Each call an act makes (a request in a batch, a real-time call, a turn of the **Sunday read**'s conversation) is kept with the act and the piece of work it was for, the request exactly as sent (instructions, schema, input, the precedents and candidate **Spans** it was shown, the model asked for), the response exactly as returned (thinking and refusals included, in the provider's own form, beside the model the provider reports, the usage and why it stopped), and what the act's checks said of it. A result the checks refused is a kept call like any other, with its reason; a request in a batch that ended without answering it is kept with no response. This is for diagnosis: **Feedback** already says which act to blame (*Feedback lands on a judgement*), and the kept call says why that act judged as it did, from what it was shown rather than from a reconstruction. **Feedback** can come weeks after the call it points at, so calls are kept for good and never rewritten, like **Feedback** as given. Each carries a hash of its request (instructions, schema, input and model), computed as recorded runs compute theirs, so a call in use and a recording share one meaning of "the same request": whether today's code would still send it is a comparison, and a call that went wrong is already a fixture's input, since every **Source** is admissible. The request ties a call to the code that made it better than a commit would: the precedents and candidates depend on the **Record** as it stood, and a commit can't say what a build from uncommitted code sent. The kept calls are a side record, like the opens and commits the register keeps: no act is shown them, they are never precedent and **Feedback** is not given on them, since an act shown its own earlier answers would be taking instructions by another route (the Model calls Guide). Usage is shown as tokens, summed per page, not as money: prices change, and a page priced from a table in the code would go stale unseen. The one place a price is worked out is a re-judging run's estimate (*Every judgement carries its judge*), which prints the date of the prices it used.

**How each thing was made is one click from it.** Each judgement links to the call that made it (a *refers to*, name or speaker to its **Item**'s reading, a relation to its relating, a gathered **Story** to the **Briefing** that gathered it, an **Assessment** to the **Sunday read**'s conversation); each call's page shows its request, its response, what the checks said, and every other attempt at that piece of work. The **Briefing**'s page and the **Sunday read**'s link to how each was written: its own call or conversation, turn by turn, and the reading and relating calls behind the **Spans** it cites, with their tokens. The **Briefing**'s is followed as it was written: **Feedback** that later took a statement out does not change what went into writing it. The **Sunday read**'s is its conversation's page, since the conversation is the whole of how the read was written: its tools read the **Record** directly, not through other calls. The Feedback page links each piece to the call behind what it is about (*missed* on a **Span**, to its **Item**'s reading and the **Briefing** that left it out), worked out by the same lookups as the things' own pages rather than stored with the piece, so the two can't disagree. Diagnosis starts from **Feedback**, which is given on judgements, often by acts upstream of the **Briefing**, so a page per output alone would leave the trail to be rebuilt by clicking back through it. These pages are checking work, not reading, so opening them is no open (*The Owner keeps reading*).

**The Editor is tested in three tiers.** Unit tests replace the model with a fake, injected rather than patched, and test first everything around the model call: typed results, the checks before writing back, tracing, and **Feedback**'s fixes to the **Record**. Recorded runs call the real model on fixture **Items**, keep its responses, and replay them in the suite, asserting properties (a pronoun *refers to* its antecedent, a quoted **Span**'s speaker is who the **Item** says spoke it, a speech *follows* the press release announcing it, a **Briefing** statement keeps its speaker's voice); a property that rests on **Feedback** names a **Feedback** fixture, made by hand from an admissible **Item**, never from a model's earlier output (a reaction like one told *unwanted* is not told; a reference like one marked *wrong* is not filled in so); they are re-recorded deliberately when an act's instructions change, and the difference shows what changed. A replay is valid only for the request it answered. Each recording keeps that request's hash: its instructions, schema and input (frozen inputs included), and the model the act is pinned to, by the one function a kept call is hashed with (for the Sunday read, one hash per turn). A replay whose act now builds a different request fails as stale, naming the command that re-records it. Without this, a change to an act's instructions leaves its old recordings green while they test a prompt that no longer exists. It fails rather than skips, because a skipped property quietly tests nothing, and a recording that keeps no hash is stale too. A turn of the **Sunday read** is hashed over its request exactly as sent, the model's earlier turns in the provider's own form, and a recording keeps each turn in that form and replays it, so the act passes back what came and the replay sends the request the recording answered. Hashing a neutral form (the turn's text and tool calls alone) would also make a recorded and a kept hash agree, but it would leave out what the provider is sent of the model's own turn, its thinking among it, so two requests the model is asked differently would hash the same, and every hash already kept would change its meaning. Whether the **Editor** judges as the **Owner** would is not a test but a reading, through **Feedback** in use. So the suite never pays for a model call; only a deliberate re-recording does. Fixtures come only from admissible **Sources**, since the suite is committed.

**Each act is recorded over its own frozen inputs.** Each act is recorded over its own frozen copies of the recordings it rests on (relating over readings, the Briefing and the Sunday read over both), never over the upstream recordings themselves, so re-recording one act leaves every other act's recordings valid, and a change to an act is re-recorded on that act alone. The copies are earlier acts' recordings, made by the production path, not hand-made **Record** fixtures: hand-made judgements would let relating pass on input no reading writes. A frozen input is part of the act's request, so refreshing it makes the act's recording stale. Refreshing is a change of its own, never part of the change to the act upstream: that act's new behaviour is accepted on its own recordings first, and then the copies are refreshed and what rests on them re-recorded, once, rather than once per fix. A refresh copies every upstream act's recordings together, because a downstream act frozen on new readings and old relations would be tested on a mix no run of the **Editor** produces. Until a refresh, the acts downstream are tested on what the earlier acts used to write. Two checks cover that gap, neither needing a recording of its own. The chain as a whole is replayed over the Briefing's frozen copies and each of its samples, each one consistent run, and asserts only what spans acts: each statement of the Briefing cites **Spans** that reading did not reject; a separately recorded chain would be one more set of recordings to keep in step, doing the same. And each act's current recordings (each fixture's first sample) are fed to the acts downstream with a fake model, asserting that their requests build and the fake's answers pass their checks, so a change to an earlier act's output that a later act can no longer take fails at once. A change in what the earlier act judges, rather than in what shape it writes, is not a failure of the code; a refresh is what brings it downstream. The Sunday read is refreshed only on the **Owner**'s say, since its re-recording costs dollars.

**A recording is one sample, and a property is judged by its pass rate.** A recorded act is stochastic, so one recording passing says only that it passed once: a property passing one try in three passes a single recording a third of the time, and that pass is luck nothing could show. So each fixture is sampled several times when it is recorded, and each property is judged by the rate at which the samples pass it. A sample is one piece of work run as it runs in use, with its attempts against the act's own checks (`MaxAttempts`), and the two kinds of property are rated apart, because they fail differently. An act's checks run in use, so a check that refuses a call costs a retry, not a wrong **Record**: the property is that the work passes within its attempts. The properties only the suite asserts (a speech *follows* its press release, a statement keeps its speaker's voice) run nowhere in use, so each failure is a wrong **Record**: they are rated over the samples that passed their checks, each call counting. Rating both per call would treat a check that costs a retry one time in three like a property that is wrong one time in three. Each property is judged per fixture, not pooled across them, because a property can fail on one **Item** (the speech) while the rate across all of them looks fine. The rule is declared before the samples are drawn. Reading, relating and the Briefing take five samples of each fixture: a property is supported at five of five, refuted at three or fewer, and undecided at four, which is read again on five fresh samples judged alone, never added to the first five. The Sunday read, at dollars a run, takes three: supported at three, refuted at one or none, undecided at two. Its sample is the read as written in use: up to `SundayReadTries` tries, each a whole conversation over a fresh copy of its frozen **Record**, kept with Wikidata's answers to its lookups, so a replay makes no network call. Each try starts from the same **Record**, so the samples share their opening turn's request; the turns after it differ with what each conversation did. No source gives these lines; they are coarse on purpose, since five samples cannot tell a rate of 80% from one of 95%, and what five of five says is only that nothing is wrong that a necessary condition can see. Every sample is committed, and the suite replays them all and judges each property by its rule on every run, so a property's status is derived from the recordings, never stored, like the register's. A rate written only into a change's description would be a claim nothing checks again, and a later change to how a property is asserted would quietly leave the samples it was rated on. A refuted or undecided property is a failing test: a refuted one until the act is changed, an undecided one until its fresh samples replace the first ones. The samples answer one request, so they share its hash. Where an act's fixtures build on each other (relating runs over the fixtures in the order they were read, so later **Spans** meet earlier ones), every sample of a fixture runs over the same **Record**: the one the fixtures before it left, each carried on by its first sample to pass its checks. A sample run over the **Record** its own earlier samples made would send a request of its own, and five such samples would be five readings of one each, not one request's rate. A frozen copy of an act's recording, for an act downstream, is its first sample, chosen before its results are seen; copies are refreshed only from recordings whose properties are supported, so that sample passed. Recorded properties are not hypotheses of the end state: they say an act behaves on fixtures, not that the **Owner** keeps reading, and their status is the suite's, not the register's. Where one serves a part of the end state it is that part's test on fixtures (the chain replay is *Everything traces* before the **Record** exists), and the part is still read from use. Whether the **Editor** judges as the **Owner** would stays the third tier, read from **Feedback**.

**Entities come from Wikidata first, otherwise from the Editor.** What a **Span** names (a person, organisation, place or named occasion) is looked up in Wikidata, and a match is used as it is. With no match, the **Editor** makes its own **Entity** (a would-be candidate, a councillor, a new campaign group); if Wikidata later gains an item for it, the two are merged and the **Editor**'s **Entity** stays as an alias, so the **Record**'s history still resolves. Wikidata gives identity, so "Andy Burnham", "the Prime Minister" and "Burnham" in three **Items** are one **Entity** without go-slow solving that matching itself. Abstract subjects are **Topics**, not **Entities**.

**The model names what a Span names; plain code finds it in Wikidata.** *Reading an Item* gives each name the name the **Item** uses, its kind, and for a person the office the **Item** gives them, in full ("the Prime Minister", office *Prime Minister of the United Kingdom*). Only a person has an office, and code drops one given to any other kind, refusing nothing, since the model fills the field whenever anything nearby describes what an **Entity** does and instructions can't hold it off. Matching is plain code over Wikidata's answers, as at the **Item**'s date, keyed on the name, kind and office (*Entities are looked up by name, not by Span*). A person with an office is sought first among the office's holders on that date (Wikidata's officeholder statements with their start and end): the one holder, when the **Item** names them only by the office, or the holder whose name contains the **Item**'s ("Burnham"). Otherwise the match is the best item Wikidata's own search ranks whose label or an alias is the name, and of the right kind (a person is an instance of *human*, nothing else is). Anything less certain is no match, and the **Editor** makes its own **Entity**. Letting the model say who "the Prime Minister" is would draw identity from its recall, which is untraced and goes stale exactly where news happens (a model trained before 20 July 2026 names the wrong Prime Minister); an office resolved by date against Wikidata is right for an **Item** from any year. Search alone fails the other way: "Burnham" finds villages before people. An **Editor**'s **Entity** is merged into a Wikidata item when a later look-up matches that item under the same name, or the item's label, so the merge needs no sweep of its own. A look-up Wikidata asks to wait for (replication lag, or its search refusing as too busy) is sent again after a backoff, a bounded number of times; one that still fails is tried again later, and holds up no reading, since the look-up is its own step.

**Entities find candidates; they don't say who did what.** A **Span** mentions an **Entity** when a **Span** inside it names the **Entity** or *refers to* one that does, and that is a signal for finding what a **Span** is weighed against: relating's candidate **Spans**, and the **Briefing**'s. Candidates are capped, and ranked by the **Entities** they share with the **Span**, each weighted by how rarely it is mentioned, so an **Entity** in nearly everything (the government, the Prime Minister) neither floods the cap nor pushes out the **Span** sharing a rarer one (Great British Railways). They are not narrowed by recency: a subject quiet for months that revives is exactly where a *follows* matters most. Entities are one signal among several (closeness in time; text similarity, not yet used), since a **Span** naming its subject only as "the city" before hydration, or not at all, still has candidates by the others. Which named thing did what to whom is not judged apart from the **Span**: a judge reads it in the **Span** itself, and roles were among the least reliable judgements the [news content research](../research/92-news-content-modelling.md) found. The speaker is the one such judgement kept, because whose words a **Span** is is often not in the **Span** at all.

**The Briefing tells what the Editor judges worth telling, in statements that cite Spans.** A **Briefing** covers the **Spans** read since the previous **Briefing** was written (the first, the day before it), so every **Span** is weighed for exactly one **Briefing** however **Collection** runs fall around the set time; taking "the day" as the calendar day of an **Item**'s date would drop one read late in the day after the **Briefing**, or read days after it was published. Nothing selects them before the **Editor** does: no **Topic** gates what is weighed, since a **Story** need not touch a **Topic** to be worth the **Owner**'s reading, and the **Briefing** leans to discovery. The **Editor** is shown the **Spans** not **Rejected**, grouped by **Item**, each with its relations: a **Span** that *follows* another moves a subject on, and one that relates to nothing may start one. It judges what to tell, gathers each told **Span**'s story (*A Story is gathered by the pass that tells it*) and writes statements, each citing every **Span** it rests on. **Spans** the *same as* one another are told once, citing them all; a statement may rest on several **Spans**, and one that condenses cites each it condenses, so *Everything traces* stays a check in code on structured citations, where tracing prose to its sources would need another model call: the check refuses the whole **Briefing** if any statement cites nothing, or cites a **Span** it was not shown. What it tells is guided by the **Owner**'s **Feedback**, through instructions developed and versioned as any act's are, and its length is its judgement, read through *unwanted*, with no bound of the **Owner**'s: a bound on reading time would cut what matters on a heavy day and pad a light one. All of a day's **Spans** go to one call while it can weigh them well; if it can't, an earlier batch step per **Item** judges which of its **Spans** are worth telling, and the **Briefing** writes over what it kept. A refused or failed **Briefing** writes nothing; the failure is kept and shown on the day's page, and writing is tried again after a quarter of an hour. With nothing read since the last, the **Briefing** is written empty and says so, with no model call. The **Briefings** written before it told **Spans** (over **Stories** of Facts, selected by **Topics**) are kept in the **Record** as they were written, an earlier shape of the same event, and their days' pages still show them. **The outputs appear** is checked on the running system: a day with no **Briefing** by the set time is flagged on its page and in the list of **Briefings**, from the first day a **Briefing** was written or tried.

**Construction and relating run as Items arrive.** After each **Collection** run, each new **Item** is cut, read and its **Spans** related, in batches; the **Briefing** is written on its own schedule over what the **Record** holds then. That keeps the **Record** current within a **Collection** run or two of an **Item** arriving, and confines a failure (a malformed **Item**, a failed model call) to one act on one **Item**, retried without holding up the **Briefing**. Nothing has to be judged again before the **Briefing**: each pair of **Spans** is judged when the second of them is read (*Relating a Span*), so a **Span** read early in the day is related to those read later by their turn, not by a second pass over its own.

**Feedback lands on a judgement, so the judge to fix is known.** *Wrong* is given on something go-slow judged: a *refers to*, an **Entity** match, a speaker, a *follows*, *same as* or *contradicts*, a gathered **Story**, a **Briefing** statement, an **Assessment**. Its effect is that every view stops reading that judgement, and it becomes precedent for the judge that made it. It is never given on a **Span**: a **Span** is the publisher's words, and a publisher being wrong is something editorial work finds as a *contradicts*, not an error of go-slow's, which ranks no **Source** or speaker by trustworthiness. A **Briefing** statement is the **Editor**'s own judgement, so it takes *wrong* itself: its prose can be wrong over **Spans** that are sound (it read "the city" as Leeds), and a statement mended only through what it cites could not be told so. *Unwanted* is given on what a **Briefing** told (the **Spans** a statement cites, as told that day), and *missed* on a **Span** the **Briefing** should have told, from its **Item**'s page or its own; both are precedent for what the **Briefing** tells. *New to me* is given on anything told (a statement, a **Story**, an **Assessment**, the **Sunday read**) and changes nothing. Adding a *follows* by hand is not offered: the **Owner** would be doing the **Editor**'s work.

**Taste fades with age; truth doesn't.** **Feedback** is point in time and carries when it was given and nothing of the state it was given against. *Unwanted* and *missed* say what the **Owner** wants told, which drifts, so as precedent they weigh less the older they are, and a **Story** that has changed since needs no account of how. *Wrong* is about a judgement, which is never redone or rewritten, so it holds at any age: a *refers to* that was wrong is wrong for good.

**Feedback becomes precedent, shown as cases.** When a judge judges, it is shown the past **Feedback** on judgements of its own kind relevant to the case (for reading and relating, found by the **Entities** the case names; for the **Briefing**, the *unwanted* and *missed* standing, newest first), a few at most, after its instructions and as data its instructions say are never instructions, so the instructions stay a stable prefix a provider can cache and nothing the **Owner** typed or an **Item** said is read as an instruction. A precedent is a case, never a rule: each act's standard is stated once, in its instructions, and what it is told about precedents appears only when it is shown some, so a case with no precedents is judged exactly as it would be without them. The precedents shown are kept with the judgement, so why a judge judged as it did can be read later from what it was shown. Editing the instructions by hand from each piece of **Feedback** would make the **Owner** the **Editor**; retraining a model on it would be slow to take effect and opaque about why the **Editor** changed. Changing an act's standard is a deliberate change to its instructions, a new version and so a new judge.

**Feedback is kept as given, and undoing it is Feedback too.** Each piece is kept as what the **Owner** said, about what, and when, apart from how it is applied, so a changed mechanism can be run over all past **Feedback** instead of starting from none. Its effect is never applied as an edit: every view works out from the **Feedback** standing what it reads, and undoing a piece is kept as its own, with when, so the piece stays and stops standing. An undone piece is never precedent and the register does not count it, since the **Owner** has said it does not hold. Editing a piece in place is not offered: the **Owner** undoes it and gives it again, so each piece stays what was said. Each page lists the **Feedback** given on what it shows, with its undo, because what *wrong* took out of a page shows nowhere else on it: a reference given *wrong* is no longer there to undo from. A look-up given *wrong* is not looked up again: the name reads as the older look-up of it had it, or as unmatched, since a fresh look-up of the same name would match the same item and stand in for the judgement the **Owner** took out.

**A written Briefing re-renders without what Feedback took out.** A **Briefing** is never rewritten, but it is shown without each statement given *wrong* and without each statement citing a **Span** told *unwanted* in it: its prose may still say what was taken out, and telling which words rest on which citation would need a model call. A **Briefing** whose statements **Feedback** took out entirely says so, and lists the **Feedback** that did, to undo, rather than reading as a quiet day.

**The Sunday read is the one place the Editor is an agent with tools.** It reads what the week's **Briefings** told, then queries the **Record** (the **Stories** of **Spans**, the **Spans** naming an **Entity**, earlier **Assessments**, back through time) and the **Reference sources** as its argument needs them. Its questions differ each week and can't be fixed in advance, so a fixed sequence would be blind to what each read needs. Its quality is read through *new to me* **Feedback**, not by testing each step.

**The Sunday read writes Assessments and tells wider stories; it keeps no Story.** What the read works out, such as "Reform's slide in the polls began before the investigation", is kept as an **Assessment** citing the **Spans** and earlier **Assessments** it rests on. That is how go-slow threads its work over time: later reads start from earlier **Assessments** instead of from scratch. An **Assessment** is a judgement, so it takes *wrong*, and it is never rewritten; a later one supersedes it, so how go-slow's view moved stays readable. A wider story (the investigation as one case of *undercover journalism into parties*) is told in the read's statements, citing the **Spans** across the **Stories** it ties together, and those **Stories** are gathered by the read as the **Briefing** gathers its own (*A Story is gathered by the pass that tells it*). A wider story kept between reads, with a title, would be a **Story** stored as a thing, which a **Story** as a view from a **Span** declines. The read writes no judgement construction makes: what a **Span** refers to, names or who speaks it is judged from its **Item** alone, and an agent's reasoning entering there would pass for what was collected.

**The read's citations are refs to what its agent was shown.** Each thing a tool shows comes with a ref (P12 a **Span**, A4 an **Assessment**, L1 a lookup of Wikidata made in this run, as the item and the revision seen), and the read is submitted through one tool, as statements each citing refs and the **Assessments** it reaches (N1, N2, each citing **Spans** and naming the current **Assessment** it supersedes, if any). The check in code refuses a submission with a statement that cites nothing, or anything the run was not shown and the read does not make, an **Assessment** citing no **Span** shown, or superseding one not shown or already superseded. Citing only what was shown, rather than anything in the **Record**, keeps an id the model recalls or guesses from passing as a citation. A refused submission is told back to the agent with the reason, so it can mend and resubmit within the run, since the work that found what to cite is in the conversation and starting again would lose it; nothing is published until a submission passes. The agent's tools only read: a **Span**'s story (its latest gathering and the relations judged since), the **Spans** naming an **Entity** back through time, earlier **Assessments**, and Wikidata's search and items. What **Feedback** took out is never shown, as to every act.

**The read's loop is bounded, and written on Sunday at the Briefing's time.** The agent may take 40 turns and spend 4 million tokens (input, cached or not, and output), checked before each turn; a read of a dozen **Stories** with a few lookups each takes well under half of either, and a loop that never converges is stopped before it costs more than a few pounds. A turn that ends without submitting is asked once to submit, since a model can be asked to call a tool but not made to. With nothing told in the week's **Briefings**, the read is written empty, with no model call. The read covers what moved on since the previous read was written (the first, the week before it), as the **Briefing** covers the day, and is written on Sunday just after the **Briefing**, at the time the **Owner** sets for it: one time is all *The outputs appear* needs, and Sunday's **Briefing** links the read. A failed read is kept and shown, and tried again an hour later, at most three times on the Sunday: it costs far more than a **Briefing**, and one that failed its checks or its bounds is likely to again.

## Telling what Spans say

**Corroboration is same as across independent Sources, and primary publishers make it the norm.** A **Span** is corroborated when it is the *same as* a **Span** of an independent **Source**; one publisher saying a thing twice (a speech and its press release) corroborates nothing. Corroboration is worked out from the *same as* judgements, never judged on its own. The text of a speech, a statutory instrument or a poll's tables outranks reports of them, and because **Collection** never searches, a primary document is in the **Record** only if its publisher is a **Source**. That is a reason to choose primary publishers (GOV.UK, Parliament, pollsters) as **Sources** alongside the outlets that report on them.

**A Span is told in its speaker's voice, and as at its date.** A **Span** is the publisher's words, so nothing in the **Record** turns what someone said into what is so; the one place that can happen is the **Briefing**'s prose, and its instructions hold it off. A **Span** with a speaker is told as theirs ("the Mayor called it fantastic news for Derby"), never in go-slow's voice. What something will do, cause or be worth (an estimate, a forecast: "the headquarters will give the region's economy a £151 million boost") is told as the projection it is, never as having happened, since as at the **Span**'s date nothing could corroborate it. An arrangement ("it will take place in Brussels on the 20th November") is told as arranged, and a declared intention as intended, since both are news as at their date and the event they look to is not yet. Each is told as at its **Item**'s date: what was true then may not be now, and a **Span** is never restated as of the present.

## How material arrives

**News material arrives only through collection.** Collection checks a fixed set of known sources on its own timetable, separate from the briefings', and lands what it finds in the record. The daily briefing and the Sunday read both work over that record; neither fetches news material of its own.

One way in keeps every request to a news source on one timetable, where politeness (request rates, conditional requests, robots.txt) is enforced in one place. A Sunday read that fetched topic-led material while it was being written would be a second, irregular visitor to the same outlets, with its own politeness to get right, and would turn collection's "check the same known sources" into topic search.

**Collection is polite by these rules.** A publisher's robots.txt is necessary but not sufficient: several allow a generic bot and forbid automated access in their terms, so a **Source** is admitted on its terms, not its robots.txt. **Collection** reads feeds and APIs, not pages. It makes conditional requests where the server supports them and otherwise polls no faster than the feed's own `ttl` or `Cache-Control`. It runs one request at a time per host, backs off the whole host on 429 or 503 for as long as `Retry-After` says, and fetches each robots.txt at most once a day. Each **Source** also has its own rate limit, the least time between two fetches of it, so an API that can't answer conditionally is asked no more often than it changes. Hosts that block scripted clients (Parliament's main site and Hansard) are never fetched, whatever their robots.txt says; their publisher is read only through its APIs. It identifies itself honestly, with a fixed User-Agent naming go-slow and a contact address, and never imitates a browser: a disguised client takes away the publisher's means of addressing it, and some terms treat circumvention as a breach. The findings behind these rules are in [sources research](../research/2-sources.md).

**The record keeps everything collected.** Every item collection brings in stays in the record, including the most that warrant nothing, and the editorial work is layered over it. Whether a detail matters can change: something passed over today becomes what a later **Span** *follows*, and is told then. With no backfill, an item dropped when it warranted nothing could never be recovered. How much of each **Item** is kept is set per **Source** by its terms, and a publisher becomes a **Source** only if its terms let the **Record** keep what the **Editor** needs: full text under an open licence (GOV.UK and Parliament under the Open Government and Open Parliament Licences), less where the terms allow less (a pollster's results with attribution). **Items** keep the personal data they carry (names in Hansard, in releases, in polls) as published. Having been published elsewhere does not stop a name being personal data; what puts the **Record** outside UK GDPR is that go-slow is private, a purely personal activity (an exemption taken from recall, not yet checked against sources). Opening go-slow to other readers would bring it under UK GDPR as well as the licences.

**An Item is collected with its full text, or not yet.** Where a **Source**'s feed carries only a summary or an opening (GOV.UK's Atom entries, Parliament's written statements list), **Collection** fetches each new entry's full text from the publisher's API (GOV.UK's Content API, the written statement itself), by the same politeness rules, and keeps the **Item** only once it has it. Every **Item** gets its text, because every **Item** is cut and read, and its **Spans** are addresses into that text: an **Item** kept with only its summary would be cut into the publisher's description of it. An entry whose text can't be fetched on this run is left out of it and collected on the next, while it is still in the feed, so an **Item** is always whole and nothing downstream (the **Cut**, reading, the pages) needs a state for one awaiting its text. *Reading an Item* is shown its text without its summary, which stands in only for an **Item** with no text: the summary is the publisher's description of the text, not more of it, and shown beside it, judgements get made on the description (a speech's "delivered a speech in Scotland today") rather than on what the speech says; the **Cut** is of the same text, so no **Span** of an **Item** with text is a stretch of its summary. Collecting the entry at once and completing it later would never miss one, but adds that state and its retries for a case only a long outage at one host makes real: an entry stays in a feed of 50 for a day or more, and a host down that long shows its **Source** quiet (*No Source goes quiet*). **Items** collected before their text was fetched are given it once, by the `backfill` admin command, only while they are unread: nothing has been judged of an unread **Item**, so giving it its text leaves no judgement pointing at an address that moved. An **Item** already read keeps the text it was read on, since its judgements are addresses into it.

**Sources are primary-led.** The **Sources** are primary publishers (government departments, Parliament, statistics bodies, pollsters, parties' own releases) and any reporting outlet whose terms permit storing and summarising its **Items**. Outlets whose terms forbid that are not collected at all: no **Items**, no headlines, no links. The major UK outlets forbid it: the BBC names summaries for one's own use and agentic AI, the Guardian bans automated reading and any AI use, and Reuters requires written consent. Being private does not change this, because UK law permits the temporary copies made while reading but not the stored copies the **Record** keeps. Keeping headline-and-link pointers to those outlets would still take the metadata the BBC's terms forbid taking. The cost is the stories that exist only as reporting, such as an undercover investigation: they reach go-slow through their consequences (resignations, statements, notices), not through the reporting itself. Building from primary material is also what makes corroboration against the primary **Item** the norm rather than the exception.

**The owner chooses the sources; topics don't.** The known sources are a list the owner maintains by hand. Topics of interest act only in the editorial work, on what collection brings in. Following a new topic may lead the owner to add a source, but nothing derives sources from topics: doing so would need source discovery from a topic, which is topic search under another name, and would let the list grow beyond what can be visited politely.

**Releases are collected; series are consulted.** A statistics body or central bank publishes two different things. A release (the ONS's GDP bulletin, an OBR forecast, the MPC's statement) is published on a date, says something and makes news: it is an **Item**, collected on the timetable, and the bodies' release calendars are **Sources** of scheduled events. A calendar entry is kept as an **Item** with the date it is due and is upcoming until then, so the **Editor** can see a release coming before it says anything; the release itself arrives later as its own **Item**. An entry moved to another date is a new **Item**, since the **Record** is never rewritten, and only its latest date is upcoming. A series (GDP by quarter, the 10-year gilt yield, sterling against the dollar) is background: it is a **Reference source**, looked up when a release or event makes it matter ("yields rose after the Budget"). A daily yield is not news, and collecting series would fill the **Record** with numbers that warrant nothing. It also keeps market data within what is openly licensed (official yield curves, reference exchange rates, policy rates), since commercial market data is licensed and not to be stored.

**Reference sources are consulted, not collected.** Wikidata, historic datasets (past polls, election results) and statistical series hold background facts about the world rather than news. The editorial work looks things up in them when it needs that background, for instance the Sunday read placing a week's poll against months of earlier ones.

**The record starts empty and is never backfilled.** Collection's first run sees only what the known sources carry that day, and the record deepens week by week. Early Sunday reads therefore have the long-run facts, from reference sources, but no earlier news: an investigation from last year is not in the record until something brings it back. Backfilling from the sources' archives would close that gap, but it is a heavy one-off visit to every outlet, the kind of fetch politeness rules out, and it would leave months of material with no editorial work done on it. The gap closes on its own as the record grows.
