# How news content is modelled (#92)

What go-slow takes from the journalism and information-science literature on modelling the content of news: the things it refers to, the happenings it reports, how the two are related, reported statements, and following a subject over time. One research pass of five researchers, briefed from the Owner's question with none of go-slow's terms, roles or proposed answers in the brief, so that it could say whether go-slow's framing was right. The working report was not committed, as the Project knowledge Guide asks; what lasts is below.

## Opinion

- **Three layers, kept apart: the thing, the reference to it, and what a statement says of it.** Every tradition read reinvents this. Newsroom standards (IPTC NewsML-G2 and ninjs, the BBC ontologies, the NYT's tagging) keep the thing in a vocabulary with its own identifier, and put only a pointer on the content, with relevance, confidence and who assigned it on the link. Information extraction keeps a mention (a span typed as name, nominal or pronoun) apart from the entity it is linked to. go-slow's language follows: an **Entity** is the thing, an **Item** *mentions* it, a **Fact** *involves* it in a **Role**.
- **What finds work should rest on the reliable layer.** Recognising what a text names is among the judgements humans agree on best (κ or agreement around .8 to .95); which of the named things a statement involves, and in what role, is much less reliable, and aboutness (what a text is about, as against what it mentions) is the least reliable judgement found, measured only through proxies. So go-slow finds what a **Fact** is weighed against by mentions, and spends the judgement of involvement only where a false tie misleads.
- **Coarse roles.** Role inventories run from two macroroles through about 21 to 25 general roles and per-predicate numbered roles to about a thousand frames, and the literature says no categorical inventory sits at a stable midpoint (Dowty, via Reisinger et al. 2015). Coarse agent/patient roles have high agreement (PropBank κ about .93, from secondary slides); event-specific ones are much lower (all five RAMS annotators agreed on whether a role is present 68.5% of the time), and zero-shot model scores fall further (GPT-4 argument extraction 7.1 F1 on WikiEvents, 24.1 on RAMS, 2023–24). go-slow's *actor* and *undergoer* are Role and Reference Grammar's two macroroles (Van Valin, *Semantic macroroles in Role and Reference Grammar*, read in full); *place* and *occasion* are the time-and-place modifiers that every event-identity criterion uses to tell happenings apart.
- **Offices are dated statements about a person, never a property of the mention.** "The Prime Minister" resolves only through a dated statement (Wikidata's *position held* with start and end), which is how the PLOVER/NGEC political event coder resolves officeholders ("the job titles that correspond with the date of the news story"). go-slow's matching already works this way.
- **A reported statement has the same shape as an established one, with its status wrapped around it; the speaker is the source of a saying, not a participant in what is said.** FactBank's factuality per chain of sources, RDF 1.2's unasserted triples, Wikidata's ranks and qualifiers and nanopublications' provenance graphs all do this, and no scheme read makes the speaker a participant in the content because they said it. go-slow's **Claim** keeps its attribution, and involves its claimant only if it gives them a role, which agrees. Whether the **Entities** inside a **Claim** should be linked like a **Fact**'s is a question every scheme decides by construction and none argues.
- **A thread is an editorial object over happenings.** TDT's topic, the BBC Storyline (with an attribution saying whose interpretation it is) and Structured Stories all keep the thread apart from the items and the happenings, which is go-slow's **Story** over **Developments**. No evaluation reports duplicate threads as a figure (a split shows only as misses), and no source says when a revived dormant thread is the same one, so go-slow's duplicates are read from its own **Feedback**.
- **Run-to-run variation is not removed by temperature.** Hosted models vary at temperature 0 (accuracy swings of up to 15% across identical runs, Atil et al.); what reduces it with evidence for extraction is detailed guidelines, decomposition and voting across samples. No study measures run-to-run variance on span extraction itself, which is why go-slow measures it on its own recordings.

Sources and their evidence quality, as the researchers read them (*read in full*; *partly read*; *page summary*: a fetched page condensed by a tool; *abstract*; *snippet*: search text only):

- Roles and events: [Aguilar et al. 2014](https://aclanthology.org/W14-2907.pdf) and [Song et al. 2015](https://www.ldc.upenn.edu/sites/www.ldc.upenn.edu/files/naacl2015-light-to-rich-ere.pdf) on ACE, ERE and FrameNet (read in full); [Reisinger et al. 2015](https://aclanthology.org/Q15-1034.pdf) on proto-roles (partly read; Dowty 1991 itself not read); [Van Valin, *Semantic macroroles in RRG*](https://rrg.caset.buffalo.edu/rrg/vanvalin_papers/SemMRsRRG.pdf) (read in full); [Halterman et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.01331) on PLOVER/NGEC (partly read); [Hovy et al. 2013](https://aclanthology.org/W13-1203.pdf) on event identity (read in full).

- Reliability: [MAVEN](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.13590) (partly read); [RAMS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.03766) and [arXiv 2404.08579](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08579v1) (snippets); [Atil et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.04667) (partly read); PropBank's κ only from [secondary slides](https://www.coli.uni-saarland.de/courses/linganno/slides/propbank.pdf) (snippet).

**Where the sources disagreed with recall.** The brief expected a settled role inventory to adopt, and there is none; the sources treat granularity as a trade-off, and one team (ERE) removed distinctions for agreement and put them back within two years. Recall also suggested that newsrooms model who did what to whom on their content; none of the newsroom models read does, keeping the content's link coarse and putting typed structure in reference data.

**What the sources leave open.** Agreement on aboutness itself, rather than a proxy; agreement on nominal mentions ("the minister") apart from names; general and event-specific roles compared on the same data, for humans or models; run-to-run variance on extraction; how to link the **Entities** inside reported content; when a revived thread is the same one; and any evaluation of 2025–26 models on these tasks. Primary sources still unread include Dowty 1991, the ACE 2005 guidelines, Palmer et al. 2005 (PropBank's κ at first hand) and the BBC's own pages.
