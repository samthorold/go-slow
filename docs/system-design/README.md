# System design

The design of go-slow: how it models the editorial work of a slow-news briefing, and why it has this shape. Terms are defined in [`CONTEXT.md`](../../CONTEXT.md). Hypotheses are stated here without status; status is in the register, derived from readings, and until the first reading every hypothesis is Due.

## End state

**The Owner keeps reading the Briefing and the Long read.** Continued use is the top because the mechanism of go-slow's value is hard to pin down: whatever it is, a reader who keeps coming back is getting it, and a reader who stops is not. go-slow sits on top of the outlets it collects from, not in place of them, so the test is use, not displacement of other news. The parts below are the faster, finer readings that say why use holds or falls.

*Reading rule.* Over a rolling 8-week window, count every day a **Briefing** is opened and every **Long read** opened.
- **Refuted**, in any window: fewer than 2 days in 7 on average, or 3 or fewer of the 8 **Long reads**. Either output being abandoned refutes it, since both are the end state.
- **Supported**, only in a quiet window (commits to go-slow on no more than 1 day in 7 on average): at least 4 days in 7, and at least 6 of the 8 **Long reads**.
- **Undecided**: anything else.

The **Owner** is also the builder, and opens the outputs to check work as well as to read them. That error inflates use, so a busy window can refute but never support: if even the builder doesn't open the **Briefing**, that is strong evidence; if the builder does, it is weak. Separating reading from checking by excluding days with commits fails while development is daily, because it excludes nearly every day. An undecided window is re-read on the next fresh window, judged alone. Review every 8 weeks once the **Briefing** runs daily.

### Necessary conditions

Three cheap, coarse conditions for any use at all. Each runs on every change and on the running system; any failure refutes, and passing confirms nothing. Without them a broken **Collection** or a missing **Briefing** would surface weeks later, as a dip in use.

- **The outputs appear.** A **Briefing** every day and a **Long read** every Sunday, by a time the **Owner** sets. Refuted by any missed day.
- **Everything traces.** Every statement of a **Briefing** or a **Long read**, and every **Assessment**, cites at least one **Span** in the **Record**, an **Assessment** or a lookup of a **Reference source**. Refuted by any that cites none. Statements with nothing behind them are the way AI news summaries fail in public (see [prior art](../research/5-prior-art.md)), and this is the mechanical guard against it.
- **No Source goes quiet.** Every **Source** yields new **Items** within its usual gap. Refuted by any **Source** silent past it without notice. The usual gap is declared with the **Source** when it is added, and silence runs from its newest **Item**'s publication, or from when collecting it began if it has yielded none, so a **Source** that never yields is caught too. Counting from publication rather than from when go-slow last collected means a **Collection** that has stopped running shows every **Source** quiet, which is the notice wanted. The Items page shows the flag.

### Finer parts

Between the top and its necessary conditions the end state has no parts yet. The top is read from opens, which need nothing of the editorial work's shape, so it reads straight through a redesign of that work. A finer part says why use holds or falls by reading what an editorial command outputs, so it is declared with the commands whose output it reads, once they have a shape worth testing; a part declared over outputs still being redrawn would be read on an instrument that changes with every redraw.

## Shape

The editorial work is the one core domain. Collection and storage are adapters around it (the Web apps Guide's ports and adapters): they fetch and keep material, and make no editorial judgement. The value of the project is the editorial work, so it is the part kept free of I/O and tested directly.

**An agent edits, and the Owner reads.** The **Editor** does the editorial work every day without waiting on the **Owner**. The **Owner** reads its output, and chooses the **Sources** and **Topics**; what the **Owner** notices about the editorial work changes its design, through issues and design sessions, not through the **Record**. A briefing the **Owner** had to edit would wait on the **Owner**'s time, the opposite of one they read. While the editorial commands are still being worked out, judgements given by the **Owner** inside the app would fix the shape of what they judge, and every command shown them as precedent would tie its recordings to rules that are changing; what the **Owner** notices changes more when it changes the commands themselves. The **Editor**'s mistakes don't compound unseen, because no judgement is final: every command reads the **Record** as it stands, so a later command judges again with what was missing before (a **Span** hydrated, an **Entity** looked up), and its events supersede the earlier ones. Its form is under *The Editor*.

**The editorial output is the same for every reader.** The **Owner**'s choices shape the editorial work, and everyone who reads the briefing reads the same one. There is no model of an audience: no per-reader selection, no segments, no engagement signal. A briefing personalised per reader would multiply the editorial work by the number of readers and turn judgement into ranking. One output keeps the editorial work the thing that is done, once.

**go-slow is private: the Owner is its only reader.** It is not publicly reachable. Publishers' terms and UK copyright law draw their sharpest line between private, personal, non-commercial use and publishing to others, so a private go-slow can use the widest set of **Sources** and keep the most of each. Opening it to other readers changes nothing in the editorial design, since the output is already the same for every reader, but it changes what the **Record** may hold and which **Sources** may be collected, and is made only once licensing allows it.

**go-slow runs when the Owner starts it.** It runs on the **Owner**'s laptop, from the same image and compose file it is developed with, and its port is published on the loopback interface only, so it stays private on any network the laptop joins: a port open on every interface would serve the **Record** to everyone else on a shared network. The **Record** is an ordinary folder on the laptop, mounted into the container, so the **Owner** can see it, copy it and keep it across resets of Docker. Nothing keeps go-slow running when the **Owner** hasn't started it, because starting it is reading it: the **Owner** starts it each day to read, and a host running it unattended would write **Briefings** nobody opens, which nothing in the end state counts. A day it isn't started is a day not read, and the top's reading already counts it as one, so it needs no rule of its own. Under *The outputs appear* as it stands, a day it isn't started has no **Briefing** by the set time and is a missed day, and so is a day it is started after the set time; whether that condition should count only the days go-slow ran is the **Owner**'s review, and until then its rule stands.

## Record construction

**The Span is the unit, and it is an address.** A **Span** is a stretch of an **Item**'s text, known by where it starts and ends, and everything go-slow says about what an **Item** says is a judgement on its **Spans**. A **Span** is never reworded: a proposition reworded from its **Item** stutters against the text it came from, and in doing so it resolves references ("the city" becomes Derby), which is a judgement of its own, invents attributions ("Andy Burnham said:" becomes the subject of every sentence after it), and loses the speaker's voice ("This is fantastic news for Derby", the Mayor's view, restated in go-slow's own). A **Span** keeps all three apart: the words are the publisher's, and what they refer to and who speaks them are judgements on them. Because the text is never rewritten, an address always points at the same words, so no judgement or **Briefing** statement is ever left pointing at something changed, and *Everything traces* is a property of the address rather than a check on a quote. The whole **Item** is not the unit either: an **Item** says several things that belong to different **Stories** (a press release moves devolution on and repeats a line about centralisation from the speech), and a relation between whole **Items** would tie everything in one to everything in the other.

**The grain belongs to the judgement, not to the Span.** **Spans** nest, from the whole **Item** to a word, and each judgement is about the stretch it needs: *refers to* is about "the city" and "Derby", a **Briefing** statement about the sentence it rests on. A **Cut** lists an **Item**'s **Spans** before anything else is judged of them (sentences, today, by plain code), so that each judge is shown the **Item** as numbered **Spans** and cites them by number, which models do reliably, where character offsets they do not; what is stored is the address the number stands for, and a judge can cite a finer stretch as words quoted within a **Span** of the **Cut**. Quoted words stand for their first occurrence in the **Span**, so a *refers to*, whose words recur (two "they"s in one sentence, referring to different things), quotes its stretch and, apart from it, words around it that locate it; only the stretch is kept. A judge that could only quote would reach a second "they" only by quoting the words around it, and the stretch kept would then hold words the judgement is not about ("they then found" refers to Manchester City Council), which hydration replaces with what they refer to. A fixed grain chosen at construction would make every later judgement work at it, and would need a judgement of its own about which **Spans** the **Briefing** consumes. The **Cut** is a judgement like the others, kept with its cutter, so a better cutter (one per **Source**, or a model) adds a **Cut** without invalidating any judgement made on an earlier one: a judgement is about an address, whichever **Cut** listed it.

**Construction judges a Span from its Item; editorial work judges over the Record at a time.** The commands fall into two kinds by what they read. *Construction* (the **Cut**, hydration, look-ups) judges a **Span** from its own **Item** alone: what it refers to and names, who speaks it, whether it is **Rejected**. It runs as each **Item** arrives, independently of every other. *Editorial work* (the **Briefing**, the **Long read**) reads the **Record** as it stands at a time, **Spans** with their **Stories**, and tells what it judges worth telling. Hydration is construction, though it needs a model, because it reads only the **Item**; and nothing waits for it: a **Span** is there for editorial work once it is cut, and one hydrated later is read hydrated by the next command that reads it.

**Hydrating a Span makes its model judgements in one call, reading its whole Item.** Shown its **Item** as its **Cut**, *hydrating a Span* judges what the **Span** *refers to*, what it names (a name and its kind), who speaks it, and whether it is **Rejected** as not useful. Each judgement is kept as its own, with the call as its judge, so a projection can take one kind from a newer judge and another from an older one. A **Span**'s four judgements are made together because they depend on each other (the speaker of "This is fantastic news for the city" is the Mayor through "he"; whether a **Span** says anything depends on what it refers to), and judged apart they could contradict each other. The unit is the **Span**, not the **Item**, because the **Span** is the unit of everything else: a **Span** a better cutter lists is hydrated without judging its **Item**'s other **Spans** again, and a **Span** judged again is judged alone. The whole **Item** is shown each time, because what a **Span** refers to and who speaks it are usually in the sentences around it; the **Item** is the same prefix for each of its **Spans**, so a provider's prompt cache carries most of the repetition.

**Entities are looked up by name, not by Span.** What a name refers to in Wikidata is looked up in plain code, keyed on the name and its kind, and for a person named by office on the office and the **Item**'s date, so "Derby" in a hundred **Spans** is one look-up, and a look-up refused or delayed holds up no reading. A look-up made per reading would repeat the same request for every **Item** naming a common **Entity**, at the one request a second Wikidata's rules allow, and one refusal would stop the reading that made it.

## The Record as events

**The Record keeps what was observed or judged; everything else is a projection.** An **Item** collected, a **Cut**, each judgement (a *refers to*, a name, a speaker, a **Rejected**, a change to a **Story**, a **Briefing**, a **Long read** and its **Assessments**) is kept as an event, at its own grain, with an identifier and the version of its shape, and is never changed. What can be worked out again from the events is a projection: the **Spans** a **Story** holds as at a time, which **Spans** mention an **Entity**, what a page shows, the register. The **Record** as it stood at a time recurs through the design: every command reads it, and a projection chooses whose judgements it reads, and a **Record** that kept only its current state could answer neither. **Sources** and **Topics** are kept as plain records, changed in place: they are configuration the **Owner** sets, not something observed or judged. Shapes are versioned because events are never rewritten: a projection reads every version of an event it has ever been given, rather than a migration rewriting history into the newest shape.

**A command reads a projection of the Record and outputs zero or more events.** Every piece of the **Editor**'s work is a command: cutting an **Item**, hydrating a **Span**, looking up a name, writing the **Briefing**, writing the **Long read**. A command reads a projection shaped for its work, gets its text through **Spans**, and appends what it judged; it never changes an event. Reading a projection rather than the history keeps each command's input bounded however long the **Record** grows, and keeps each command self-contained: no command waits for another to have run, or knows which ran before it. So the **Record** is eventually consistent. A command run again over a **Record** that has moved on (a **Span** hydrated since, an **Entity** looked up) can judge differently, and that is intended: it is how judgements improve as the **Record** fills in. Commands are decoupled on purpose; ordering them (hydration before any editorial work, say) would make the slowest command hold up every command after it. An event can lead to a command (an **Item** collected is cut; a **Span** cut is hydrated), and commands that lead to each other could run without end; no chain of them runs away, by a timetable or otherwise.

**The Record is recreated when the design of its judgements changes, not translated.** When the judgements the **Record** keeps change in kind, the judgements made in the old kinds are left in the **Record** as events no projection reads, and the commands are run again over the **Items**: before the **Span** was the unit, Facts and Claims reworded from each **Item**, and later relations between **Spans** (*follows*, *same as*, *contradicts*) and the **Stories** gathered from them. None is carried into the new kinds: a Fact is a rewording, which a **Span** declines (*The Span is the unit*), and a relation turned into **Stories** in code would chain them, which **Stories** as groups decline (*A Story is a group of Spans*); and the design was changing too fast for those judgements to be worth translating (the **Owner**'s call). What carries through is what was observed rather than judged: the **Items**, with their text and when they were collected; the days a **Briefing** or **Long read** was written or failed, and when the **Owner** opened them, which the register reads; the commits; and the kept calls and conversations, which are never removed. Each switch is kept as an event. Running the commands again over the **Items** collected before a switch calls the model over the whole **Record**, so it is a run the **Owner** starts, priced first (*Every judgement carries its judge*), never work the next start takes up unasked; and an **Item** collected before a switch is never told by a **Briefing** as today's (*The Briefing tells only Items collected after the switch*).

**Text is kept apart from the events, so it can be forgotten.** An **Item**'s text is stored beside the events, which refer to it by **Item** and address, so a **Source**'s terms that require forgetting an **Item** are met by removing its text, while the events stay append-only and their addresses point into text that is gone. Forgetting has to reach every copy: the kept calls and the recordings hold text too. Every **Source** today is under the Open Government or Open Parliament Licence with full text kept, so nothing is forgotten yet. An address is into the text as kept, the publisher's HTML, not into a plain-text rendering of it, since a rendering is code that can change and would move every address with it; the words at an address are read by dropping its tags. An **Item** collected with only its summary is not cut until its text is fetched, so no **Cut** addresses text that changes after it.

**Every judgement carries its judge, and a changed judge judges forward.** A judge is the command, its instructions' version, the model and the request's hash, as kept calls record. A new version of a command is a new judge: it judges what arrives from then on, and the judgements made before stand, with their judge. A projection reads, for each thing judged, the newest judge that has judged it, so the **Record** mixes judges over time, and every page and the register can say which judge made what they show. Re-judging the whole **Record** with every change would cost as much as the **Record** is large, growing daily, for every instruction tried; re-judging a range is a deliberate run on the **Owner**'s say, priced first, as a re-recording is. A candidate judge is compared on the live **Record** by re-judging a sample, kept beside the live judgements and read by no projection until it is chosen; since **Spans** are addresses that never change, no judgement is ever left pointing at nothing by a judge that judged differently. Choosing a candidate is itself an event, and rewrites none of its judgements: from the choice on, the candidate is a live judge whose judgements count as made when it was chosen, so they are newer than every live judgement made before the choice, and a live judgement made after it is newer still. Ordering them by when they were made instead would let a live judge that judged the same thing between the candidate's run and its choice outrank the judge just chosen. A candidate is chosen only on the model and version the command runs on from then, so the judge the **Owner** chose is the one that goes on judging what arrives. A re-judging run calls the model in real time, one request at a time, though a batch costs half: the run is a one-off the **Owner** starts and watches end, and a batch would either hold it open for up to a day or leave its results to the app's timetable, which applies only work awaiting a command, not work judged again. Its price is estimated before it runs, in dollars at real-time rates, from a price table kept with the model adapter and dated, and the dry run prints the date, so a stale table shows where it is used. Re-judging reads only **Items** cut, so a run first cuts the **Items** in its range that have text and no Cut, as Collection would (plain code, no model call). Those Cuts are the dry run's only write. The run stops before a call that could take it over the most it may spend, since the estimate is only an estimate.

## Editorial work

**A Story is a group of Spans kept in the Record.** A **Story** has an identity of its own, and the **Spans** it holds as at a time are a projection of the events that change it. Every **Span** belongs to at least one **Story**, and can belong to several. Kept, a **Story** is the same thing to every command that reads it: what yesterday's **Briefing** left is what today's starts from, and a **Span** no command has told is in a **Story** all the same. A **Story** gathered afresh by each command that tells it would hold nothing between two gatherings, so two days could gather one subject differently, and a **Span** told before its neighbours arrived would be in no **Story** at all. A **Story** drifts as new **Spans** arrive, and that is how it learns: a subject that moves on is the **Story** moving on. What keeps **Stories** sound is not a rule against drift but that editorial work never stops: every command that reads **Stories** can change them, so a grouping is always being re-formed by a reader that sees it whole. A large **Story** and a **Topic** can look alike, and that is fine: a **Topic** is the **Owner**'s terms and selects nothing, a **Story** is the **Editor**'s grouping.

**An Item gives its Spans their first Story.** When an **Item** is cut, plain code puts its **Spans** into one new **Story**: an **Item**'s **Spans** mostly belong together, or the **Item** would be incoherent. So every **Span** is in a **Story** from the moment it is cut, at no model call and waiting on nothing. After that the **Item** links nothing: **Stories** do, and the editorial work separates a **Span** that belongs elsewhere, or brings the **Item**'s **Story** together with others.

**No relations between Spans are judged.** Whether two **Spans** say the same thing, contradict each other, or one moves the other on is noticed by the editorial work when it reads a **Story**'s **Spans** together, and goes into what it tells. Judged pair by pair, each would cost a call per pair and leave a chain for **Stories** to drift along, joining two subjects through any **Span** that says two things, until most of the **Record** is one. Order in time comes from publication; causation in the world is the **Long read**'s to reason about, in its **Assessments**.

**Editorial commands change Stories as they work.** The **Briefing** and the **Long read** read **Spans** with their **Stories**, and as commands they output, beside what they tell, the events that change **Stories**: which **Spans** a **Story** holds, and how **Stories** stand to one another (a **Long read**'s wider story among them, kept as a **Story** over the **Stories** it ties together). Changing a **Story** is where it is read whole, beside its neighbours, so the reader best placed to see that one **Item**'s **Story** belongs with last week's is the one telling it. A **Story** nothing tells keeps the shape its **Item** gave it, and nothing reads it. The **Owner** changes no **Story** by hand.

## The Editor

**The Editor's daily work is a set of commands.** Each is a bounded step with a typed result, reading a projection of the **Record** and writing back only what passes its checks:

1. *Cutting an Item* lists its **Spans**, and puts them in the **Item**'s first **Story**, in plain code.
2. *Hydrating a Span* judges what it refers to, what it names, who speaks it and whether it is **Rejected**; plain code then looks the names up in Wikidata.
3. *Writing the Briefing* judges what to tell of the **Spans** of the **Items** cut since the last, writes statements citing them, and changes **Stories**.
4. *Writing the Long read* reasons over the week's **Briefings** and the **Record**, writes **Assessments** and statements, and changes **Stories**.

A command is a model call where judgement is needed and plain code where it is not. A single agent with tools over the **Record**, deciding for itself what to do each day, fails on three counts. When an output is wrong, the command that made it is known, and so what to fix; with one agent, only that the agent got worse. "Everything traces" becomes a property of each command's result (a judgement is about addresses in its **Item**) rather than something an agent must remember. And each command can be tested first, on recorded **Items**, by properties such as "a quoted **Span**'s speaker is who the **Item** says spoke it" and "a **Briefing** statement keeps its speaker's voice". The **Long read** is the one exception (*The Long read is the one place the Editor is an agent with tools*).

**Every Item is cut and hydrated, and Topics select nothing.** Every **Item** collected is cut and its **Spans** hydrated, whatever it is about. What the **Owner** reads is the **Editor**'s judgement (*The Briefing tells what the Editor judges worth telling*), and it leans to discovery: a **Story** need not touch a **Topic** to be worth telling, and a gate on **Topics** would hide exactly what the **Owner** didn't know to follow. Hydrating everything is cheap enough not to plan around at go-slow's volume, and keeping selection out of construction means a **Span** is judged the same way whatever anyone follows, so no **Item** waits on interest that changes. The **Owner** steers instead through the **Sources**: what is shown per **Source** is how many of the **Stories** it contributes to touch a **Topic** and how many don't, and how many of its **Spans** are **Rejected**, and the **Owner** prunes **Sources** on it. A **Span** touches a **Topic** by the **Topic**'s terms, matched as whole words in the text a reader sees, with no model; that counts, and selects nothing. A **Story** a **Source** contributes to is one that holds, as it stands, a **Span** of one of its **Items**, and it touches a **Topic** when one of its **Spans** does. The counts are projections over a stated window, the last four weeks (the **Stories** holding a **Span** of an **Item** collected in it, the **Spans** of those **Items**), worked out on every view and never stored, so taking up or removing a **Topic** changes them at once and changes nothing else. Four weeks is long enough for a **Source** that publishes weekly to show a few **Stories**, and short enough to show a **Source** as it is now rather than as it was. A **Topic** has terms, since terms are the only way a **Span** touches one: a **Topic** given only **Sources** would say where **Items** come from, which the counts already show. There is no **Depth**: how closely a **Topic** is followed decided what was read and told, and both are now the **Editor**'s judgement.

**The model is an adapter, and the one adapter is Claude.** Every command that calls a model does so through a provider-neutral port, and each command owns its instructions and output schema, so the commands are tested against a fake and each command's model is a configuration value. The one adapter is Claude through Anthropic's API, and which Claude model runs which command is chosen on the recorded fixtures by their properties ([model research](../research/models.md)). Open-weight models are not used. From an inference provider they are paid per token like Claude, and at go-slow's volume (about 100 daily calls) every candidate costs a few dollars a month, so the most they could save is less than a second adapter, a second set of recordings for every command and a measurement between providers cost to build and keep. Where they would be free at the margin, a model the **Owner** runs, they need a machine running all day, and the models such a machine holds are not the frontier ones. The port stays provider-neutral, so a second adapter stays a new integration rather than a redesign, if the cost of the calls ever outgrows the cost of the work. Each command runs on a pinned model ID, one whose weights the provider never changes, so behaviour can't change between two readings, or under a green recording, without a change in go-slow; an alias the provider moves to a newer model would change the command while every recording still passed. A model's ID is part of the command's request, so changing it is a re-record, like changing the command's instructions. Each result keeps the model the provider reports answering with, not the one asked for, so if a configured ID ever did move, the **Record** would show it.

**A provider never trains on go-slow's requests and keeps them only for a limited, stated period.** Most of what is sent is public, but what go-slow asks about over months (which **Sources**, which **Stories** told) is a profile of what the **Owner** follows. Strict zero retention would shut out most closed models' standard terms for little gain over a profile of interests; a provider that trains on requests or keeps them indefinitely is excluded.

**Hydration runs in batches; the Briefing is written in real time.** Nothing waits on a single **Span**, and the design already accepts up to a **Collection** run between an **Item** arriving and its **Spans** being hydrated, so hydration is submitted together and processed asynchronously, which is cheaper where a provider discounts batches, as Anthropic does. Writing the **Briefing** is one request just before it is due, so it runs at once. A batch is never waited on: after each **Collection** run the results of batches that have ended are applied, through each command's own checks, and then everything awaiting a command and not already in a batch is submitted as one batch. Results are matched to their work by the identifier each request was given, never by position, since a provider returns them in any order. Work stays awaiting its command until its result is written, and the pending batches are kept in the **Record**, so a restart picks them up and a result is applied to the work as it stands when it arrives. A result for work that would now be asked otherwise (its projection has moved since the request was sent) answers a question no longer the work's, so it is refused and the work asked again as it stands. A request that failed, expired or fails its command's checks is resubmitted alone, so one bad **Span** confines its failure to itself, as *Construction runs as Items arrive* requires, rather than waiting for the next batch or holding one up.

**No command waits for another, the Briefing included.** Nothing is collected while go-slow is stopped, so a morning start collects everything since the last run, and its hydration goes out in batches that can take hours to end. The **Briefing** is written at the set time over what the **Record** holds then, and never waits on them: a **Span** is there for editorial work once its **Item** is cut, which is plain code done as the **Item** is collected, and it is in its **Item**'s **Story** from then, so the overnight **Items** are in the morning's **Briefing** whether or not their hydration has ended. A **Span** told before it is hydrated is told with less (no *refers to*, no speaker), and that is accepted: the **Owner** prefers a **Briefing** on time over a better one late, and the next command that reads it reads it hydrated. Waiting for the start's batches is what made a **Briefing** miss its time, and a bound on the wait only chooses how late. On Sundays the **Long read** follows the **Briefing** as always.

**The Briefing tells only Items collected after the switch.** A **Briefing** is shown the **Spans** of **Items** first cut since the previous one, and that stands for **Items** that are new. The **Items** the **Record** held at a switch are not: all are cut again, or first cut, when the commands run again over them (*The Record is recreated*), and told by when they were cut, the next **Briefing** would be handed the whole **Record** at once as today's news, in one request no one priced. So an **Item** collected before the switch is never among the **Spans** a **Briefing** tells as today's, whenever it was cut or hydrated. It stays in the **Record** as an earlier **Span**: today's **Spans** reach it through their **Stories**, the **Long read**'s tools read it, and it has its page. A **Record** with no switch (one that held no **Items** when it changed) tells every **Item** it cuts.

**Work that keeps failing is given up after three attempts, and taken up again by hand.** Each piece of work (one command on one **Span** or **Item**) gets three attempts across its batches, the first and each resubmission alike; after the third failure it is marked failed in the **Record**, with when and why it last failed, and is no longer submitted. A batch that has not ended, or cannot be polled, two days after it was submitted (twice the day within which a provider ends one) is given up, and counts a failed attempt for each piece of work in it, so a batch that disappears at the provider or never ends doesn't leave its work pending for good. A result that came back but fails to be applied for a reason other than the command's checks (a Wikidata lookup refused, the **Record** failing) is not an attempt: the attempt is the model call, and that call answered. It stops only that result: the batch's other results are applied in the same run, the batch is kept holding only the requests still to apply, so their work is not submitted again, and each run applies them again from the kept results, at no model call, until they apply. The line between waiting and counting is the batch's deadline: a result still failing to apply when its batch is two days old counts a failed attempt, as a lost batch's work does, so a fault that never passes ends in the work being marked failed and listed, not waiting for good. Stopping the whole run at such a result would hold up every result behind it and everything new waiting to be submitted, on every run, for one passing refusal. The cap is there because every attempt is a model call: a request whose answer always fails its checks, or is always refused, would otherwise be paid for on every **Collection** run without bound, and a failure that persists across three attempts, minutes to hours apart, is one in the **Span** or the command, not in the provider, so a fourth attempt is unlikely to fare better. Failed work no longer awaits its command, so nothing waits behind it. Failed work is listed on the **Items** page with its reason rather than dropped silently, since a **Span** never hydrated is a gap in the **Record** the **Owner** should know of. Retrying is by hand, through the `retry` admin command, which clears the failed marks and resets the attempts: what fixes failed work is a change the system can't see (a command's instructions or checks, a provider's outage, a malformed **Item** corrected at its **Source**), so retrying on a timer would only spend the calls the cap saves, and the **Owner** is the one who knows the cause has been dealt with.

**Every model call is kept, as it was sent and as it came back.** Each call a command makes (a request in a batch, a real-time call, a turn of the **Long read**'s conversation) is kept with the command and the piece of work it was for, the request exactly as sent (instructions, schema, input, the projection it was shown, the model asked for), the response exactly as returned (thinking and refusals included, in the provider's own form, beside the model the provider reports, the usage and why it stopped), and what the command's checks said of it. A result the checks refused is a kept call like any other, with its reason; a request in a batch that ended without answering it is kept with no response. This is for diagnosis: when an output looks wrong, the command that made it is known, and the kept call says why it judged as it did, from what it was shown rather than from a reconstruction. A mistake can be noticed weeks after the call that made it, so calls are kept for good and never rewritten. Each carries a hash of its request (instructions, schema, input and model), computed as recorded runs compute theirs, so a call in use and a recording share one meaning of "the same request": whether today's code would still send it is a comparison, and a call that went wrong is already a fixture's input, since every **Source** is admissible. The request ties a call to the code that made it better than a commit would: the projection it was shown depends on the **Record** as it stood, and a commit can't say what a build from uncommitted code sent. The kept calls are a side record, like the opens and commits the register keeps: no command is shown them, since a command shown its own earlier answers would be taking instructions by another route (the Model calls Guide). Usage is shown as tokens, summed per page, not as money: prices change, and a page priced from a table in the code would go stale unseen. The one place a price is worked out is a re-judging run's estimate (*Every judgement carries its judge*), which prints the date of the prices it used.

**How each thing was made is one click from it.** Each judgement links to the call that made it (a *refers to*, name or speaker to its **Span**'s hydration, a change to a **Story** to the **Briefing** or **Long read** that made it, an **Assessment** to the **Long read**'s conversation); each call's page shows its request, its response, what the checks said, and every other attempt at that piece of work. The **Briefing**'s page and the **Long read**'s link to how each was written: its own call or conversation, turn by turn, and the hydration calls behind the **Spans** it cites, with their tokens. The **Long read**'s is its conversation's page, since the conversation is the whole of how the read was written: its tools read the **Record** directly, not through other calls. Diagnosis starts from an output that looks wrong, often made by a command upstream of it, so a page per output alone would leave the trail to be rebuilt by clicking back through it. A **Story**'s page shows the **Spans** it holds as they stand, in publication order (its **Timeline**), and each **Span**'s page the **Stories** it is in. These pages are checking work, not reading, so opening them is no open (*The Owner keeps reading*).

**The Editor is tested in three tiers.** Unit tests replace the model with a fake, injected rather than patched, and test first everything around the model call: typed results, the checks before writing back, and tracing. Recorded runs call the real model on fixture **Items**, keep its responses, and replay them in the suite, asserting properties (a pronoun *refers to* its antecedent, a quoted **Span**'s speaker is who the **Item** says spoke it, a **Briefing** statement keeps its speaker's voice); they are re-recorded deliberately when a command's instructions change, and the difference shows what changed. A replay is valid only for the request it answered. Each recording keeps that request's hash: its instructions, schema and input (frozen inputs included), and the model the command is pinned to, by the one function a kept call is hashed with (for the **Long read**, one hash per turn). A replay whose command now builds a different request fails as stale, naming the command that re-records it. Without this, a change to a command's instructions leaves its old recordings green while they test a prompt that no longer exists. It fails rather than skips, because a skipped property quietly tests nothing, and a recording that keeps no hash is stale too. A turn of the **Long read** is hashed over its request exactly as sent, the model's earlier turns in the provider's own form, and a recording keeps each turn in that form and replays it, so the command passes back what came and the replay sends the request the recording answered. Hashing a neutral form (the turn's text and tool calls alone) would also make a recorded and a kept hash agree, but it would leave out what the provider is sent of the model's own turn, its thinking among it, so two requests the model is asked differently would hash the same, and every hash already kept would change its meaning. Whether the **Editor** judges well is not a test but a reading, in use, through the end state. So the suite never pays for a model call; only a deliberate re-recording does. Fixtures come only from admissible **Sources**, since the suite is committed.

**Each command is recorded over its own frozen inputs.** Each command is recorded over its own frozen copies of the recordings it rests on (the **Briefing** over hydration's, the **Long read** over both), never over the upstream recordings themselves, so re-recording one command leaves every other command's recordings valid, and a change to a command is re-recorded on that command alone. The copies are earlier commands' recordings, made by the production path, not hand-made **Record** fixtures: hand-made judgements would let the **Briefing** pass on input no hydration writes. A frozen input is part of the command's request, so refreshing it makes the command's recording stale. Refreshing is a change of its own, never part of the change to the command upstream: that command's new behaviour is accepted on its own recordings first, and then the copies are refreshed and what rests on them re-recorded, once, rather than once per fix. A refresh copies every upstream command's recordings together, because a downstream command frozen on a mix no run of the **Editor** produces would be tested on that mix. For the same reason refreshes go in chain order, the **Briefing** before the **Long read**: a command's recordings answer its own frozen copies, so the **Briefing**'s current recordings fit hydration's current recordings only once the **Briefing** has been refreshed onto them and re-recorded. Until a refresh, the commands downstream are tested on what the earlier commands used to write. Two checks cover that gap, neither needing a recording of its own. The chain as a whole is replayed over the **Briefing**'s frozen copies and each of its samples, each one consistent run, and asserts only what spans commands: each statement of the **Briefing** cites **Spans** that hydration did not reject; a separately recorded chain would be one more set of recordings to keep in step, doing the same. And each command's current recordings (each fixture's first sample) are fed to the commands downstream with a fake model, asserting that their requests build and the fake's answers pass their checks, so a change to an earlier command's output that a later command can no longer take fails at once. A change in what the earlier command judges, rather than in what shape it writes, is not a failure of the code; a refresh is what brings it downstream. The **Long read** is refreshed only on the **Owner**'s say, since its re-recording costs dollars.

**A recording is one sample, and a property is judged by its pass rate.** A recorded command is stochastic, so one recording passing says only that it passed once: a property passing one try in three passes a single recording a third of the time, and that pass is luck nothing could show. So each fixture is sampled several times when it is recorded, and each property is judged by the rate at which the samples pass it. A sample is one piece of work run as it runs in use, with its attempts against the command's own checks (`MaxAttempts`), and the two kinds of property are rated apart, because they fail differently. An command's checks run in use, so a check that refuses a call costs a retry, not a wrong **Record**: the property is that the work passes within its attempts. The properties only the suite asserts (a statement keeps its speaker's voice) run nowhere in use, so each failure is a wrong **Record**: they are rated over the samples that passed their checks, each call counting. Rating both per call would treat a check that costs a retry one time in three like a property that is wrong one time in three. Each property is judged per fixture, not pooled across them, because a property can fail on one **Item** (the speech) while the rate across all of them looks fine. The rule is declared before the samples are drawn. Hydration and the Briefing take five samples of each fixture: a property is supported at five of five, refuted at three or fewer, and undecided at four, which is read again on five fresh samples judged alone, never added to the first five. The Long read, at dollars a run, takes three: supported at three, refuted at one or none, undecided at two. Its sample is the read as written in use: up to `SundayReadTries` tries, each a whole conversation over a fresh copy of its frozen **Record**, kept with Wikidata's answers to its lookups, so a replay makes no network call. Each try starts from the same **Record**, so the samples share their opening turn's request; the turns after it differ with what each conversation did. No source gives these lines; they are coarse on purpose, since five samples cannot tell a rate of 80% from one of 95%, and what five of five says is only that nothing is wrong that a necessary condition can see. Every sample is committed, and the suite replays them all and judges each property by its rule on every run, so a property's status is derived from the recordings, never stored, like the register's. A rate written only into a change's description would be a claim nothing checks again, and a later change to how a property is asserted would quietly leave the samples it was rated on. A refuted or undecided property is a failing test: a refuted one until the command is changed, an undecided one until its fresh samples replace the first ones. The samples answer one request, so they share its hash. Where an command's fixtures build on each other, every sample of a fixture runs over the same **Record**: the one the fixtures before it left, each carried on by its first sample to pass its checks. A sample run over the **Record** its own earlier samples made would send a request of its own, and five such samples would be five readings of one each, not one request's rate. A frozen copy of an command's recording, for an command downstream, is its first sample, chosen before its results are seen; copies are refreshed only from recordings whose properties are supported, so that sample passed. Recorded properties are not hypotheses of the end state: they say an command behaves on fixtures, not that the **Owner** keeps reading, and their status is the suite's, not the register's. Where one serves a part of the end state it is that part's test on fixtures (the chain replay is *Everything traces* before the **Record** exists), and the part is still read from use. Whether the **Editor** judges well stays the third tier, read in use.

**Entities come from Wikidata first, otherwise from the Editor.** What a **Span** names (a person, organisation, place or named occasion) is looked up in Wikidata, and a match is used as it is. With no match, the **Editor** makes its own **Entity** (a would-be candidate, a councillor, a new campaign group); if Wikidata later gains an item for it, the two are merged and the **Editor**'s **Entity** stays as an alias, so the **Record**'s history still resolves. Wikidata gives identity, so "Andy Burnham", "the Prime Minister" and "Burnham" in three **Items** are one **Entity** without go-slow solving that matching itself. Abstract subjects are **Topics**, not **Entities**.

**The model names what a Span names; plain code finds it in Wikidata.** *Hydrating a Span* gives each name the name the **Item** uses, its kind, and for a person the office the **Item** gives them, in full ("the Prime Minister", office *Prime Minister of the United Kingdom*). Only a person has an office, and code drops one given to any other kind, refusing nothing, since the model fills the field whenever anything nearby describes what an **Entity** does and instructions can't hold it off. Matching is plain code over Wikidata's answers, as at the **Item**'s date, keyed on the name, kind and office (*Entities are looked up by name, not by Span*). A person with an office is sought first among the office's holders on that date (Wikidata's officeholder statements with their start and end): the one holder, when the **Item** names them only by the office, or the holder whose name contains the **Item**'s ("Burnham"). Otherwise the match is the best item Wikidata's own search ranks whose label or an alias is the name, and of the right kind (a person is an instance of *human*, nothing else is). Anything less certain is no match, and the **Editor** makes its own **Entity**. Letting the model say who "the Prime Minister" is would draw identity from its recall, which is untraced and goes stale exactly where news happens (a model trained before 20 July 2026 names the wrong Prime Minister); an office resolved by date against Wikidata is right for an **Item** from any year. Search alone fails the other way: "Burnham" finds villages before people. An **Editor**'s **Entity** is merged into a Wikidata item when a later look-up matches that item under the same name, or the item's label, so the merge needs no sweep of its own. A look-up Wikidata asks to wait for (replication lag, or its search refusing as too busy) is sent again after a backoff, a bounded number of times; one that still fails is tried again later, and holds up no hydration, since the look-up is its own step.

**Entities find candidate Stories; they don't say who did what.** A **Span** mentions an **Entity** when a **Span** inside it names the **Entity** or *refers to* one that does, and that is a signal for finding which **Stories** a command reads beside the ones in front of it: the **Briefing**'s, and the **Long read**'s tools. Candidates are capped, and ranked by the **Entities** their **Spans** share with the **Spans** at hand, each weighted by how rarely it is mentioned, so an **Entity** in nearly everything (the government, the Prime Minister) neither floods the cap nor pushes out the **Story** sharing a rarer one (Great British Railways). They are not narrowed by recency: a subject quiet for months that revives is exactly where an earlier **Story** matters most. **Entities** are one signal among several (closeness in time; text similarity, not yet used), since a **Span** naming its subject only as "the city" before hydration, or not at all, still has candidates by the others. Which named thing did what to whom is not judged apart from the **Span**: a judge reads it in the **Span** itself, and roles were among the least reliable judgements the [news content research](../research/92-news-content-modelling.md) found. The speaker is the one such judgement kept, because whose words a **Span** is is often not in the **Span** at all.

**The Briefing tells what the Editor judges worth telling, in statements that cite Spans.** A **Briefing** covers the **Spans** of the **Items** first cut since the previous **Briefing** was written (the first, the day before it), so every **Span** is weighed for exactly one **Briefing** however **Collection** runs fall around the set time; taking "the day" as the calendar day of an **Item**'s date would drop one collected late in the day after the **Briefing**, or collected days after it was published. Nothing selects them before the **Editor** does: no **Topic** gates what is weighed, since a **Story** need not touch a **Topic** to be worth the **Owner**'s reading, and the **Briefing** leans to discovery. The **Editor** is shown the **Spans** not **Rejected**, with the **Stories** they are in as they stand, and candidate **Stories** beside them (*Entities find candidate Stories*). It judges what to tell, writes statements, each citing every **Span** it rests on, and outputs the changes to **Stories** it judged on the way. Reading a **Story**'s **Spans** together is where it notices what they do to each other: two **Spans** saying the same thing are told once, citing both; a contradiction is told as one; and whether a thing said twice is corroborated depends on whether its **Sources** are independent (a speech and its own press release are one publisher, so not), which its instructions say. A statement may rest on several **Spans**, and one that condenses cites each it condenses, so *Everything traces* stays a check in code on structured citations, where tracing prose to its sources would need another model call: the check refuses the whole **Briefing** if any statement cites nothing, or cites a **Span** it was not shown. What it tells is the judgement its instructions describe, developed and versioned as any command's are, and its length is its judgement, with no bound of the **Owner**'s: a bound on reading time would cut what matters on a heavy day and pad a light one. All of a day's **Spans** go to one call while it can weigh them well. A refused or failed **Briefing** writes nothing; the failure is kept and shown on the day's page, and writing is tried again after a quarter of an hour. With nothing cut since the last, the **Briefing** is written empty and says so, with no model call. Of a **Briefing** written before a switch, only its day carries through (*The Record is recreated*), and its page says what it said is gone. **The outputs appear** is checked on the running system: a day with no **Briefing** by the set time is flagged on its page and in the list of **Briefings**, from the first day a **Briefing** was written or tried.

**Construction runs as Items arrive.** After each **Collection** run, each new **Item** is cut, its **Spans** put in its first **Story** and submitted for hydration, in batches; the **Briefing** is written on its own schedule over what the **Record** holds then. That keeps the **Record** current within a **Collection** run or two of an **Item** arriving, and confines a failure (a malformed **Item**, a failed model call) to one command on one **Span**, retried without holding up the **Briefing**.

**The Long read is the one place the Editor is an agent with tools.** It reads what the week's **Briefings** told, then queries the **Record** (the **Stories** of **Spans**, the **Spans** naming an **Entity**, earlier **Assessments**, back through time) and the **Reference sources** as its argument needs them. Its questions differ each week and can't be fixed in advance, so a fixed sequence would be blind to what each read needs. Its quality is read in use, not by testing each step.

**The Long read writes Assessments and keeps wider stories.** What the read works out, such as "Reform's slide in the polls began before the investigation", is kept as an **Assessment** citing the **Spans** and earlier **Assessments** it rests on. That is how go-slow threads its work over time: later reads start from earlier **Assessments** instead of from scratch. An **Assessment** is never rewritten; a later one supersedes it, so how go-slow's view moved stays readable. A wider story (the investigation as one case of *undercover journalism into parties*) is told in the read's statements, citing the **Spans** across the **Stories** it ties together, and kept as a **Story** over those **Stories**, so the next read, and the next **Briefing**, start from it. The read writes no judgement construction makes: what a **Span** refers to, names or who speaks it is judged from its **Item** alone, and an agent's reasoning entering there would pass for what was collected.

**The read's citations are refs to what its agent was shown.** Each thing a tool shows comes with a ref (P12 a **Span**, A4 an **Assessment**, L1 a lookup of Wikidata made in this run, as the item and the revision seen), and the read is submitted through one tool, as statements each citing refs and the **Assessments** it reaches (N1, N2, each citing **Spans** and naming the current **Assessment** it supersedes, if any). The check in code refuses a submission with a statement that cites nothing, or anything the run was not shown and the read does not make, an **Assessment** citing no **Span** shown, or superseding one not shown or already superseded. Citing only what was shown, rather than anything in the **Record**, keeps an id the model recalls or guesses from passing as a citation. A refused submission is told back to the agent with the reason, so it can mend and resubmit within the run, since the work that found what to cite is in the conversation and starting again would lose it; nothing is published until a submission passes. The agent's tools only read: a **Span**'s **Stories** as they stand, the **Spans** naming an **Entity** back through time, earlier **Assessments**, and Wikidata's search and items. Of a read written before a switch, only its day carries through, as of a **Briefing**.

**The read's loop is bounded, and written on Sunday at the Briefing's time.** The agent may take 40 turns and spend 4 million tokens (input, cached or not, and output), checked before each turn; a read of a dozen **Stories** with a few lookups each takes well under half of either, and a loop that never converges is stopped before it costs more than a few pounds. A turn that ends without submitting is asked once to submit, since a model can be asked to call a tool but not made to. With nothing told in the week's **Briefings**, the read is written empty, with no model call. The read covers what moved on since the previous read was written (the first, the week before it), as the **Briefing** covers the day, and is written on Sunday just after the **Briefing**, at the time the **Owner** sets for it: one time is all *The outputs appear* needs, and Sunday's **Briefing** links the read. A failed read is kept and shown, and tried again an hour later, at most three times on the Sunday: it costs far more than a **Briefing**, and one that failed its checks or its bounds is likely to again.

## Telling what Spans say

**Corroboration is the Briefing's to notice, and primary publishers make it the norm.** A thing is corroborated when independent **Sources** say it; one publisher saying it twice (a speech and its press release) corroborates nothing. The **Briefing** notices it when it reads a **Story**'s **Spans** together, as it notices repetition and contradiction, and no code works it out. The text of a speech, a statutory instrument or a poll's tables outranks reports of them, and because **Collection** never searches, a primary document is in the **Record** only if its publisher is a **Source**. That is a reason to choose primary publishers (GOV.UK, Parliament, pollsters) as **Sources** alongside the outlets that report on them.

**A Span is told in its speaker's voice, and as at its date.** A **Span** is the publisher's words, so nothing in the **Record** turns what someone said into what is so; the one place that can happen is the **Briefing**'s prose, and its instructions hold it off. A **Span** with a speaker is told as theirs ("the Mayor called it fantastic news for Derby"), never in go-slow's voice. What something will do, cause or be worth (an estimate, a forecast: "the headquarters will give the region's economy a £151 million boost") is told as the projection it is, never as having happened, since as at the **Span**'s date nothing could corroborate it. An arrangement ("it will take place in Brussels on the 20th November") is told as arranged, and a declared intention as intended, since both are news as at their date and the event they look to is not yet. Each is told as at its **Item**'s date: what was true then may not be now, and a **Span** is never restated as of the present.

## How material arrives

**News material arrives only through collection.** Collection checks a fixed set of known sources on its own timetable, separate from the briefings', and lands what it finds in the record. The daily briefing and the Long read both work over that record; neither fetches news material of its own.

One way in keeps every request to a news source on one timetable, where politeness (request rates, conditional requests, robots.txt) is enforced in one place. A Long read that fetched topic-led material while it was being written would be a second, irregular visitor to the same outlets, with its own politeness to get right, and would turn collection's "check the same known sources" into topic search.

**Collection is polite by these rules.** A publisher's robots.txt is necessary but not sufficient: several allow a generic bot and forbid automated access in their terms, so a **Source** is admitted on its terms, not its robots.txt. **Collection** reads feeds and APIs, not pages. It makes conditional requests where the server supports them and otherwise polls no faster than the feed's own `ttl` or `Cache-Control`. It runs one request at a time per host, backs off the whole host on 429 or 503 for as long as `Retry-After` says, and fetches each robots.txt at most once a day. Each **Source** also has its own rate limit, the least time between two fetches of it, so an API that can't answer conditionally is asked no more often than it changes. Hosts that block scripted clients (Parliament's main site and Hansard) are never fetched, whatever their robots.txt says; their publisher is read only through its APIs. It identifies itself honestly, with a fixed User-Agent naming go-slow and a contact address, and never imitates a browser: a disguised client takes away the publisher's means of addressing it, and some terms treat circumvention as a breach. The findings behind these rules are in [sources research](../research/2-sources.md).

**The record keeps everything collected.** Every item collection brings in stays in the record, including the most that warrant nothing, and the editorial work is layered over it. Whether a detail matters can change: something passed over today joins a **Story** a later **Span** is told in, and is told then. With no backfill, an item dropped when it warranted nothing could never be recovered. How much of each **Item** is kept is set per **Source** by its terms, and a publisher becomes a **Source** only if its terms let the **Record** keep what the **Editor** needs: full text under an open licence (GOV.UK and Parliament under the Open Government and Open Parliament Licences), less where the terms allow less (a pollster's results with attribution). **Items** keep the personal data they carry (names in Hansard, in releases, in polls) as published. Having been published elsewhere does not stop a name being personal data; what puts the **Record** outside UK GDPR is that go-slow is private, a purely personal activity (an exemption taken from recall, not yet checked against sources). Opening go-slow to other readers would bring it under UK GDPR as well as the licences.

**An Item is collected with its full text, or not yet.** Where a **Source**'s feed carries only a summary or an opening (GOV.UK's Atom entries, Parliament's written statements list), **Collection** fetches each new entry's full text from the publisher's API (GOV.UK's Content API, the written statement itself), by the same politeness rules, and keeps the **Item** only once it has it. Every **Item** gets its text, because every **Item** is cut and hydrated, and its **Spans** are addresses into that text: an **Item** kept with only its summary would be cut into the publisher's description of it. An entry whose text can't be fetched on this run is left out of it and collected on the next, while it is still in the feed, so an **Item** is always whole and nothing downstream (the **Cut**, hydration, the pages) needs a state for one awaiting its text. *Hydrating a Span* is shown its **Item**'s text without its summary, which stands in only for an **Item** with no text: the summary is the publisher's description of the text, not more of it, and shown beside it, judgements get made on the description (a speech's "delivered a speech in Scotland today") rather than on what the speech says; the **Cut** is of the same text, so no **Span** of an **Item** with text is a stretch of its summary. Collecting the entry at once and completing it later would never miss one, but adds that state and its retries for a case only a long outage at one host makes real: an entry stays in a feed of 50 for a day or more, and a host down that long shows its **Source** quiet (*No Source goes quiet*). **Items** collected before their text was fetched are given it once, by the `backfill` admin command, only while they are uncut: every judgement is about an address a **Cut** listed, so nothing has been judged of an uncut **Item**, and giving it its text leaves no judgement pointing at an address that moved. An **Item** already cut keeps the text it was cut from, since its judgements are addresses into it.

**Sources are primary-led.** The **Sources** are primary publishers (government departments, Parliament, statistics bodies, pollsters, parties' own releases) and any reporting outlet whose terms permit storing and summarising its **Items**. Outlets whose terms forbid that are not collected at all: no **Items**, no headlines, no links. The major UK outlets forbid it: the BBC names summaries for one's own use and agentic AI, the Guardian bans automated reading and any AI use, and Reuters requires written consent. Being private does not change this, because UK law permits the temporary copies made while reading but not the stored copies the **Record** keeps. Keeping headline-and-link pointers to those outlets would still take the metadata the BBC's terms forbid taking. The cost is the stories that exist only as reporting, such as an undercover investigation: they reach go-slow through their consequences (resignations, statements, notices), not through the reporting itself. Building from primary material is also what makes corroboration against the primary **Item** the norm rather than the exception.

**The owner chooses the sources; topics don't.** The known sources are a list the owner maintains by hand. Topics of interest act only in the editorial work, on what collection brings in. Following a new topic may lead the owner to add a source, but nothing derives sources from topics: doing so would need source discovery from a topic, which is topic search under another name, and would let the list grow beyond what can be visited politely.

**Releases are collected; series are consulted.** A statistics body or central bank publishes two different things. A release (the ONS's GDP bulletin, an OBR forecast, the MPC's statement) is published on a date, says something and makes news: it is an **Item**, collected on the timetable, and the bodies' release calendars are **Sources** of scheduled events. A calendar entry is kept as an **Item** with the date it is due and is upcoming until then, so the **Editor** can see a release coming before it says anything; the release itself arrives later as its own **Item**. An entry moved to another date is a new **Item**, since the **Record** is never rewritten, and only its latest date is upcoming. A series (GDP by quarter, the 10-year gilt yield, sterling against the dollar) is background: it is a **Reference source**, looked up when a release or event makes it matter ("yields rose after the Budget"). A daily yield is not news, and collecting series would fill the **Record** with numbers that warrant nothing. It also keeps market data within what is openly licensed (official yield curves, reference exchange rates, policy rates), since commercial market data is licensed and not to be stored.

**Reference sources are consulted, not collected.** Wikidata, historic datasets (past polls, election results) and statistical series hold background facts about the world rather than news. The editorial work looks things up in them when it needs that background, for instance the Long read placing a week's poll against months of earlier ones.

**The record starts empty and is never backfilled.** Collection's first run sees only what the known sources carry that day, and the record deepens week by week. Early Long reads therefore have the long-run facts, from reference sources, but no earlier news: an investigation from last year is not in the record until something brings it back. Backfilling from the sources' archives would close that gap, but it is a heavy one-off visit to every outlet, the kind of fetch politeness rules out, and it would leave months of material with no editorial work done on it. The gap closes on its own as the record grows.
